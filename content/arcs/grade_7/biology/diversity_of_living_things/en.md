## Stage 1: Hook

### Hook - Story 1: The Mystery of the Forest Ecosystem

Nova walks into a forest on a field trip. She sees moss, insects, mushrooms, plants, and some birds. She hears a buzzing sound, then sees a bee, followed by a small spider and a slug on the bark of a tree. "Wow," she says, noticing how many things are happening here. Her teacher says, "We don't usually see all these living things in one place. It's like nature’s big puzzle — we have to sort them out." 

Then she shows Nova a sheet of paper with a list of terms: Domain, Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species. She explains each term is a way to organize life on Earth.

"This is how we classify living things. But how did scientists first think of this division? Why is it important? Let's start exploring."

### Hook - Story 2: The Science of Health: What's Hiding in Your Cold?

Nova is at the doctor's office; he's examining her for a cold. She notices he uses a tool that shows different types of microorganisms — some are rod-shaped, others are round. "This is your body fighting an infection," he says, pointing to one organism. "It's a bacteria – it can make you sick." She notices it's from the Kingdom Monera. The doctor points to another shape and says, "This is a fungus – not good to have in your lungs, either. It's a pathogen." Then he explains how each type of microorganism causes different diseases depending on its classification. "We organize all living things in a system so we can understand what we're fighting."

Nova then realizes how important it is to know where things belong in the world of living things – and why.

## Stage 2: Concept

### Concept: Classification System and Kingdoms

Nova opens her science notebook and begins exploring the classification of living things. She reviews the hierarchy: **Domain → Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species**. For each level, she explains the concept with simple visuals and Canadian examples.

She introduces her audience:

"Scientists classify living things using specific steps. These help them organize life and find patterns in nature. The hierarchy looks like this: [Domain][Kingdom][Phylum][Class][Order][Family][Genus][Species]."

She then lists the **five kingdoms** and shows key examples:

- **Monera (Bacteria)**: Tiny single-celled organisms not found in soil, they cause diseases and some make food.
- **Protista**: Mostly single-celled organisms. They include algae and amoebas. Some cause diseases in plants and animals.
- **Fungi**: Plants that have no leaves or roots — they get nutrients by breaking down dead things. They help the ecosystem survive.
- **Plantae**: Green plants with leaves. They make their own food and breathe, unlike animals. They are all non-living creatures.
- **Animalia**: Creatures that move, eat other things, and don’t make food. They live in many habitats.

Nova continues by showing a chart of **binomial nomenclature**, which helps humans identify organisms using two Latin names — one for the genus (like *Homo*) and one for the species (*sapiens*). 

Nova concludes: "We sort living things into kingdoms so we know how they function and interact with each other — and to help treat diseases and protect biodiversity."

### Comprehension Check

Nova shows the audience a chart showing the five kingdoms and one example. She asks:
"What kingdom would a mushroom belong to?"

A. Monera  
B. Protista  
C. Fungi  
D. Animalia  

**Correct Answer**: C. Fungi

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1**: "Think about what mushrooms eat – do they get nutrients from breaking down dead things?"
- **Level 2**: "Which kingdom includes things that don't make their own food but absorb it from dead materials?"
- **Level 3**: "Fungi are heterotrophs that decompose. They are not plants or animals but a separate branch of life."

## Stage 3: Guided Problem

### Guided Problem: Identifying the Kingdom of an Organism

Nova says: "Let’s look at this organism – a single-celled creature that has a flagellum, lives in pond water, and can form spores. What kingdom might it be part of?"

A. Animalia  
B. Fungi  
C. Protista  
D. Plantae  

**Correct Answer**: C. Protista

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1**: "What does it move with, and is it a plant or animal?"
- **Level 2**: "Which kingdom is made up of mostly one-celled life forms that are neither plants nor animals?"
- **Level 3**: "Protists are mostly microscopic – and many of them move via flagella. That’s their key clue!"

### Guided Problem: Classifying a Human Being

Nova adds: "Now let’s try classifying a human – what kingdom and phylum does humanity belong to?"

A. Plantae, Angiosperms  
B. Animalia, Chordata  
C. Protista, Mammalia  
D. Fungi, Basidiomycota  

**Correct Answer**: B. Animalia, Chordata

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1**: "Do humans make their own food or eat other living things?"
- **Level 2**: "Is the human body made of cells with a backbone?"
- **Level 3**: "Humans are animals, and their phylum Chordata includes all animals with a backbone – like frogs, fish, and birds."

## Stage 4: Practice

### Practice Problem 1: Dichotomous Key Challenge

Nova presents a **dichotomous key** problem. She says: "Let’s identify a creature using the steps of a dichotomous key. Does the organism have fur or feathers?"

1. If fur → goes to Step 2  
2. If feathers → goes to Step 3  

Step 2: Does it feed on insects or plants?  
- Feeds on insects → is a **bat**  
- Feeds on plants → is a **squirrel**  

Step 3: Are feathers soft and light or thick?  
- Light and soft → **cardinal**  
- Thick → **eagle**  

Nova presents: "Which organism did the key lead to if it had feathers and thick ones?"

A. Cardinal  
B. Eagle  
C. Squirrel  
D. Bat  

**Correct Answer**: B. Eagle

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1**: "Which step in the key helped narrow it down to feathered ones?"
- **Level 2**: "If it had thick feathers, which animal would be in that group?"
- **Level 3**: "A dichotomous key works in pairs of choices. You only take the branch that matches the organism’s trait."

### Practice Problem 2: Bacteria vs. Fungi vs. Protista

Nova says: "Which organism can be both helpful and harmful in our daily lives?"

A. Bacteria in our gut  
B. Mushrooms in the forest  
C. Algae in a lake  
D. Yeast used to make bread  

**Correct Answer**: D. Yeast used to make bread

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1**: "Which of these is used in food making and can also cause spoilage?"
- **Level 2**: "Which one makes food in your kitchen, but also makes some foods ferment?"
- **Level 3**: "Yeast helps in baking and brewing, but is also a microorganism that can cause food poisoning if misused."

### Practice Problem 3: Biodiversity and Classification

Nova presents the final practice: "A study is done in Canada to see how many animal species live in the forest. What scientific purpose does this serve?"

A. To know what’s not there  
B. To understand how to protect biodiversity  
C. To count only the biggest animals  
D. To prove that all species are equal  

**Correct Answer**: B. To understand how to protect biodiversity

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1**: "What do we want to learn more about if we study many species?"
- **Level 2**: "By studying diversity, how do we protect animals in nature?"
- **Level 3**: "Knowing how many species are in a forest helps conservationists and ecologists make plans to protect fragile ecosystems."

## Stage 5: Capstone

### Capstone Problem: Organism Mapping

Nova gives a challenge: "You're in a Canadian forest and find two new organisms. You identify them using a dichotomous key and classify them using the biological hierarchy. First, a small, round, non-moving organism with a cell wall, no nucleus, and is found on rotting logs. Second, a creature with a backbone, feathers, and builds nests. What kingdom and phylum do each belong to?"

**Part 1**: Identify the kingdom of the first creature  
A. Protista  
B. Fungi  
C. Monera  
D. Animalia  

**Part 2**: Identify the phylum of the second creature  
A. Chordata  
B. Arthropoda  
C. Reptilia  
D. Mammalia  

**Correct Answers**:  
Part 1: C. Monera  
Part 2: A. Chordata  

**Interventions:**
- **Part 1**: "Does it have a nucleus? Does it make its own food or break down dead things?"  
- **Part 2**: "Does it have a backbone? What type of organism has feathers?"
