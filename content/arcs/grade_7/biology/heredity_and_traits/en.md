## Stage 1: Hook — Heredity and Traits

**Story:** 

**Hook 1: Eye Color**

In a small town in Ontario, a group of kids are trying to figure out who is "the most like their parents" in terms of eye color. One student, Alex, has green eyes, which he got from his mother who also has green eyes. His father, however, has brown eyes. Alex’s little sister has green eyes and is curious to know if there is a science behind eye color. She is excited to learn that her eye color comes from her parents’ genes.

**Hook 2: Tongue Rolling**

In another classroom in Quebec, students are discovering how their genes affect everyday actions. A boy named Marc is showing off how he can roll his tongue like his grandfather, even though his younger sister can’t. The class is trying to understand why some people can roll their tongues and others can’t — and why sometimes these traits are passed down to children.

Both stories tie into real-world experiences students can relate to and spark curiosity about heredity.

## Stage 2: Concept — Heredity and Traits

**Teaching Content:**
- **Genes** = segments of DNA that influence traits
- **Alleles** = different versions of a gene
- **Dominant allele** = the trait that appears even if only one copy is present
- **Recessive allele** = the trait that only appears if two copies are present
- **Genotype** = the genetic makeup (letters like "Bb")
- **Phenotype** = the physical trait (eye color, rolling tongue)
- **Homozygous** = having two identical alleles (like "BB" or "bb")
- **Heterozygous** = having two different alleles (like "Bb")
- **Punnett Square** = visual tool to predict genetic outcomes
- **Inherited vs Environmental traits** = traits coming from parents vs being influenced by environment (e.g., a tan from sun exposure)

**Comprehension Check:**

**Question:** A child has a trait that only appears when two copies of the gene are present. What is this trait called?

A) Dominant  
B) Recessive  
C) Genotype  
D) Phenotype  

**Interventions:**

- **Level 1:** "Remember, a trait that needs two genes to show up is...?"
- **Level 2:** "Look at the difference — dominant traits show up with just one gene copy. This trait requires two copies."
- **Level 3:** "A recessive trait always needs two copies. If a gene only shows up when both copies are there, it's ___. What's the word?"

**Correct Answer:** B) Recessive

## Stage 3: Guided — Punnett Square Practice

**Problem:**

In this simple plant, tall stems (T) are dominant over short stems (t). A plant with a genotype of Tt is crossed with another plant with a genotype of Tt. What is the probability that their offspring will have a tall stem?

A) 25%  
B) 50%  
C) 75%  
D) 100%  

**Interventions:**

- **Level 1:** "Start with the parents. One plant has genotype Tt, the other also Tt. What are the alleles they can pass on?"
- **Level 2:** "If both parents are Tt, what are the possible combinations of alleles for the offspring?"
- **Level 3:** "Draw a Punnett square with T and t from one parent, and T and t from the other. Then count how many of the four squares have a tall stem (T_)."

**Correct Answer:** C) 75%

## Stage 4: Practice — Punnett Squares and Concepts

**Problem 1:**

A mother has a genotype of Rr (round seeds) and a father has a genotype of rr (wrinkled seeds). What is the chance that their child will have wrinkled seeds?

A) 0%  
B) 25%  
C) 50%  
D) 100%  

**Interventions:**

- **Level 1:** "The father can only give 'r' alleles. The mother can give 'R' or 'r'. What are the possible results?"
- **Level 2:** "The father has genotype rr, so he can only give r. The mother is Rr, so she can give R or r. What are the outcomes?"
- **Level 3:** "Punnett square: Mother Rr and father rr. How many outcomes show wrinkled seeds (rr)?"

**Correct Answer:** C) 50%

**Problem 2:**

If two heterozygous plants (Tt) are crossed, what are the odds that a randomly selected offspring will be homozygous dominant?

A) 0%  
B) 25%  
C) 50%  
D) 75%  

**Interventions:**

- **Level 1:** "Two Tt plants crossed. What are the combinations in the Punnett square?"
- **Level 2:** "How many boxes with TT are there in the Punnett square?"
- **Level 3:** "In a Punnett square for Tt x Tt, how many squares show TT out of four?"

**Correct Answer:** B) 25%

**Problem 3:**

An organism's genotype might be Bb (brown eyes dominant to blue eyes). What would the phenotype be?

A) Blue eyes  
B) Brown eyes  
C) Green eyes  
D) Both brown and blue eyes  

**Interventions:**

- **Level 1:** "Dominant traits show even with just one gene copy. B is dominant over b."
- **Level 2:** "Brown eyes are dominant. Bb means a person has a dominant B and a recessive b. What do they look like?"
- **Level 3:** "A Bb organism has a dominant brown allele. What trait is expressed?"

**Correct Answer:** B) Brown eyes

## Stage 5: Capstone — Heredity and Genetics

**Problem:**

A dog breeder wants to breed two dogs. One has a genotype of Bb (black fur dominant to brown fur), and the other too. What is the probability that their offspring will have black fur?

A) 25%  
B) 50%  
C) 75%  
D) 100%  

**Guiding Question (between attempts 1 and 2 only):**

> "Think about what the two parents can pass on. The Punnett square gives you the answer."

**If passed:**

> "You’ve used Punnett squares to model heredity successfully. That means you understand dominant and recessive traits, genotypes, and how alleles are passed on. This is advanced science — and you've done it!"

**If failed after 2 attempts:**

> "This problem needed two steps – using Punnett squares and calculating percentages. It’s hard, and you're learning something new. Let’s try one more time next time. You're doing great!"

**Alternate Capstone:**

**Problem:**

A child is born with blue eyes, while both parents have brown eyes. The child's genotype is bb (blue eyes are recessive), but both parents are Bb (brown eyes). What does this tell us?

A) The parents can't have a blue-eyed child  
B) One of the parents has a different genotype  
C) The child got two recessive genes  
D) The child's genotype was changed  

**Interventions for Alternate Capstone (if needed):**

- **Level 1:** "If both parents are Bb and have brown eyes, where did the child get two b genes?"
- **Level 2:** "The child has blue eyes. What does that mean about the genes?"
- **Level 3:** "Both parents could give the child a b. So b + b = bb. What does this show?"

**Correct Answer:** C) The child got two recessive genes

- Confirm that the language around genetic diseases is accurate, factual, and not alarmistic.
- Check that example genotypes and phenotypes used in the arc (e.g., eye color, tongue rolling) are culturally and scientifically appropriate.
- Confirm that vocabulary such as "dominant" and "recessive" is clearly explained and understood before introducing Punnett squares.
- Ensure the language used remains clear and avoids technical jargon for Grade 7 learners.
