## Stage 1: Hook  
### [Topic Key]: cell_division_and_growth  
### [Hook Story: 1]  

**Title:** "How Do You Grow Up?"  
**Story:** 

Imagine Emma, a 12-year-old, has just grown 2 inches taller this year. How is it possible? Is Emma’s body getting bigger because her body parts are just growing larger? Actually, no — her body gets bigger by making **more** cells. Every time her bones grow longer, or her muscles get stronger, or her organs work harder, her body is making **new cells** to do the job. But how do these brand-new cells get made?

In one of her science class experiments, Emma found out that her body is constantly making new cells to grow. She looked at a sample of skin under a microscope and saw that the cells were **changing shape** and **splitting apart** to make copies. That process is called **cell division**. Emma wonders — why does the body need to grow cells all the time, and how does it know how to build the new cells?

**How does the body know what to build in each new cell? What does DNA do in all those new cells? When cells divide, is there a special plan or instruction manual?**

---

### [Hook Story: 2]  

**Title:** "Healing Wounds Fast"  
**Story:** 

One morning, Liam was playing soccer in the schoolyard. He fell and cut his knee. Right away, his body began to heal himself. The skin and tissue around the cut grew back to cover it up. How did this happen? His body made new cells to grow back the damaged skin — but how did it know to **make exactly the right skin cells** to fix his knee?

Liam’s science project showed him how his body was able to **make new body parts**. He learned that this **cell regeneration** isn't just about growing more cells, but choosing the right type to do the job — like the skin cells to fix a cut, the muscle cells to heal a muscle, or nerve cells to restore sensation.

His teacher told him that all new cells start with the **same basic instruction manual** inside them — this is **DNA**. DNA is copied every time a cell divides, so each new cell knows exactly what role it has to play.

Liam was amazed — his body is **like a factory**, with instructions for making different cells to build bones, skin, muscle, and more. And when a cell divides, it’s not random — it follows a special **plan**.

## Stage 2: Concept  
### [Topic Key]: cell_division_and_growth  
### [Comprehension Check]  

**How does growth happen in your body?**

A) Your body parts just get larger  
B) Your body makes more cells  
C) Your body gets taller and wider, but not more cells  
D) Your body stops making cells  

**Answer:** B  

**Common misconception distractors:**  
A) Suggests cells grow in size instead of multiplying  
C) Refers to body shape change without considering cellular growth  
D) Incorrectly assumes no new cells are made  

**Intervention Hints (for comprehension check):**  
- Level 1: "Think about how you grew last year — did your body get bigger because your arms got longer or because there were more cells?"  
- Level 2: "Your body needs to build more cells to grow — imagine if you had only 1 cell in your arm, would you be able to get stronger or longer?"  
- Level 3: "If a plant is growing, it makes more leaves and roots — what does your body do?"  

### [Concept Summary]  

**Why do cells divide?**  
- To grow  
- To repair damages (like wounds)  
- To replace old or damaged cells  

**What is mitosis?**  
Mitosis is the process where one cell **splits into two identical cells**. It happens so that the body can make more cells when needed.  

**What is DNA?**  
DNA is the instruction manual of the cell. It tells every new cell what to become and how to work.  

**How does the cell divide?**  
DNA is copied and then the cell **divides into two**. This ensures that every new cell has a complete set of the same instructions.

## Stage 3: Guided  
### [Topic Key]: cell_division_and_growth  
### [Guided Problem]  

**Question:**  
A skin cell divides so that it can repair a cut. What happens during mitosis that helps the repair?

A) The cell grows bigger  
B) The cell copies its DNA and splits into two identical cells  
C) The cell creates new DNA instructions  
D) The cell dies and is replaced by another  

**Answer:** B  

**Common misconception distractors:**  
A) Confuses physical growth with division  
C) Incorrectly says new DNA instructions are made  
D) Believes healing involves cell death instead of division  

**Intervention Hints:**  
- Level 1: "Think about a cut you’ve had — how do you know it heals? What is happening to the cells to make that happen?"  
- Level 2: "What happens in every cell every time it divides — does it make a copy of itself?"  
- Level 3: "How does your body know what to put into the new cells to make skin when a wound heals?"

## Stage 4: Practice  
### [Topic Key]: cell_division_and_growth  
### [Practice Problem 1]  

**Question:**  
Where does the information come from that tells a new cell what part of the body it should become?

A) From other cells nearby  
B) From DNA in the new cell  
C) From the amount of nutrients in the body  
D) From the temperature in your body  

**Answer:** B  

**Common misconception distractors:**  
A) Suggests external influence instead of internal instruction  
C) Incorrectly refers to nutrition instead of DNA  
D) Incorrectly connects body temperature to instructions  

**Intervention Hints:**  
- Level 1: "What would happen if a skin cell didn’t know what it was supposed to be? Where would it get that information?"  
- Level 2: "How does DNA act like a recipe? It tells each cell what to build — for example, muscle or brain cells."  
- Level 3: "What happens in a cell when it divides? Does it copy itself or just change its shape?"  

---

### [Practice Problem 2]  

**Question:**  
Which of the following best describes what happens during mitosis?

A) Cell splits but DNA does not copy  
B) DNA copies, and then cell splits into two  
C) Cell merges with another cell  
D) DNA and cell both die in the process  

**Answer:** B  

**Common misconception distractors:**  
A) Incorrectly says DNA doesn’t copy  
C) Suggests fusion instead of division  
D) Believes mitosis destroys cells  

**Intervention Hints:**  
- Level 1: "When a cell divides, what happens to the DNA inside it? Does it copy first or does it leave?"  
- Level 2: "Imagine splitting a sandwich in half — the same idea is happening in a cell!"  
- Level 3: "After DNA copies itself, what does the cell do next to form two new cells?"  

---

### [Practice Problem 3]  

**Question:**  
Which of the following is NOT a reason why cells divide?

A) To repair damage  
B) To grow the body  
C) To reproduce sex cells  
D) To replace old cells  

**Answer:** C  

**Common misconception distractors:**  
A) Incorrectly includes reproduction instead of growth and repair  
B) This is a correct reason  
D) Another correct reason  

**Intervention Hints:**  
- Level 1: "What are two main jobs of cell division — growing up or fixing cuts?"  
- Level 2: "How does the body make new skin? Does it grow by splitting cells or does it need a special process for making babies?"  
- Level 3: "Think of what happens inside a wound — it's all about cell repair and growth, not reproduction."

## Stage 5: Capstone  
### [Topic Key]: cell_division_and_growth  
### [Capstone Problem]  

**Question:**  
A child has a cut on their arm. Which of the following processes helps the body repair the cut?

A) The body stops cell division  
B) The body divides cells to create skin cells  
C) The body uses more fat cells to replace the cut  
D) The body replaces the skin by splitting the cut into two  

**Answer:** B  

**Common misconception distractors:**  
A) Incorrectly says cells stop dividing  
C) Suggests fat is used instead of skin cells  
D) Incorrectly describes how the cut heals  

**Intervention Hints:**  
- Level 1: "If you have a cut, what needs to grow back to cover it? What kind of cell do you need?"  
- Level 2: "Skin cells are made by splitting other skin cells — what role does cell division play in healing?"  
- Level 3: "When a cell splits, does it make a copy of itself or change its shape?"  

**Bonus Thought Question (Not graded):**  
If cells are dividing all the time in your body, how does the body not get too big?

## [End of Arc – Summary]  
In this unit, students learned:  
1. The body grows by creating more cells.  
2. Each new cell gets a copy of the DNA — the instruction manual.  
3. Mitosis is the process in which a cell divides into two identical cells.  
4. Cells divide to grow, repair, and replace old or damaged cells.
