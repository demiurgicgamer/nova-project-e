## Stage 1: Hook
**Hook Story 1**

Imagine you’re on a roller coaster, speeding around curves, falling downward, and shooting upward. Your body feels heavier when going down and lighter when going up. How does the coaster car move so fast and then slow down without any engines? 

You're not just moving — **you're changing energy!** At the top of a hill, your car has *stored energy* called **potential energy**. When it starts going down, that potential energy turns into *motion energy* called **kinetic energy**. At the bottom, you have maximum kinetic energy and minimal potential energy.

This is just one example of how energy changes and moves around us!

**Hook Story 2**

It’s a calm, sunny day near a hydroelectric dam in British Columbia. The water stored behind the dam has a lot of **potential energy** because it’s sitting at a high elevation. When the water flows through the dam, it spins a turbine. That spinning turbine generates electricity — turning **potential energy** into **electrical energy**!

Energy transforms all around us, even in places like dams we see every day.

## Stage 2: Concept
**Concept Summary**: Energy is the ability to do work or make things move. When an object moves due to a force, it can do work. There are two main types of energy:
- **Kinetic Energy (KE):** The energy of motion.
- **Potential Energy (PE):** Stored energy due to an object's position or state.

The main types include:
- **Gravitational Potential Energy**: Energy due to height above the ground.
- **Work**: The transfer of energy by applying a force over a distance ($W = F \times d$).

Total energy in a system is conserved — that means energy changes forms, but it doesn't just disappear or appear.

3. Only kinetic energy is real.

## Stage 3: Guided
**Guided Problem:**

A skateboarder is at the top of a hill that is 3 meters high. They start moving, and as they go down, their speed increases. Which of the following best describes the energy transformation during this motion?

1. Kinetic energy increases, potential energy decreases.
2. Potential energy increases, kinetic energy decreases.
3. Both energies remain the same.
4. Kinetic energy increases, potential energy increases.

✅ Correct Answer: **1. Kinetic energy increases, potential energy decreases.**

**Interventions:**

## Stage 5: Capstone
**Capstone Problem:**

A hiker climbs a 50-meter tall mountain. The hiker has a mass of 60 kg. Use the formula for gravitational potential energy $PE = mgh$ (where $g ≈ 10 \, m/s²$) to calculate the gravitational potential energy of the hiker at the top of the mountain. Then, when the hiker starts descending, how much energy is converted to kinetic energy if 10% of the initial potential energy is lost due to friction?

- A) 30,000 J, 27,000 J  
- B) 60,000 J, 54,000 J  
- C) 3,000 J, 2,700 J  
- D) 5,000 J, 4,500 J  

✅ Correct Answer: **A) 30,000 J, 27,000 J**

**Step-by-step breakdown:**
1. **Potential energy calculation**:
   - $PE = mgh = 60 \, kg \times 10 \, m/s² \times 50 \, m = 30,000 \, J$
2. **Kinetic energy after 10% loss due to friction**:
   - Energy loss = $30,000 \, J \times 0.10 = 3,000 \, J$
   - Remaining kinetic energy = $30,000 \, J - 3,000 \, J = 27,000 \, J$
