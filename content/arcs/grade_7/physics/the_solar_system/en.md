## Stage 1: Hook
### Story A: The Night Sky Over Canada
Imagine you're outside in the freezing cold of winter in Vancouver, BC. You look up, and you see the Moon high in the sky. The sky is so dark that stars are visible, too. You know the stars are far away, but you wonder: how far away are they? And why can't you see the Sun during this part of the night?

Now, let's talk about what is up there above us — a place called the Solar System. It's where we live — in our home planet, Earth! You might not realize it, but we're not the only thing spinning in space around the Sun. There are planets, moons, comets, asteroids, and gases — all in their own orbits. But how do they stay in motion? What gives them their orbits?

### Story B: The Sun's Power
Picture yourself in a place like Yellowknife, Northwest Territories — a long, dark winter night, far from any city lights. You're looking at something very strange: even in darkness, the sky shows small lights — but not stars. You're seeing the aurora borealis — Northern Lights!

Why do these lights appear in Canada and not in other places? Why is Earth, the only planet that has life, so special?

Let’s explore the world of space, the Sun, planets, and how Earth fits in the bigger picture. We're going to understand why everything orbits, how the Moon cycles, and how all this affects the world we live in.

## Stage 2: Concept
### The Solar System: Understanding What We See
Before we start diving into planets, let's get the idea: what is the solar system?

The solar system consists of the Sun and everything that orbits around it, including planets, moons, comets, and asteroids.

We have 8 planets in our solar system, listed in order from closest to farthest from the Sun:
1. Mercury
2. Venus
3. Earth
4. Mars
5. Jupiter
6. Saturn
7. Uranus
8. Neptune

Each planet is different in size and distance from the Sun:
- Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun.
- Earth has one natural satellite, the Moon.
- Jupiter is the largest planet.
- Saturn is known for its beautiful ring system.
- Uranus and Neptune are called "ice giants" due to their composition.
  
Each planet orbits the Sun because of gravity.

Why does gravity pull planets in orbit around the Sun? Gravity is the force that pulls two objects toward each other. The Sun is so large that it pulls the planets towards it, but they don't crash into the Sun. Instead, they keep orbiting around it like a spinning top – they have enough sideways motion to stay away from the Sun, but gravity constantly pulls them inward.

### Key Concept Check:
Which planet is the farthest from the Sun?

A. Earth  
B. Mars  
C. Neptune  
D. Jupiter  

### Intervention Hints (for comprehension check):
- Level 1: Remember that the question asks for the farthest, not the closest.
- Level 2: What's the correct order from the Sun?
- Level 3: Think about what comes last in the solar system – what planet is not in the first four?

✅ Correct answer: C. Neptune

## Stage 3: Guided Problem
### Problem:
Which of the following correctly lists the first four planets of the solar system in order, starting from the Sun?

A. Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars  
B. Earth, Venus, Mercury, Mars  
C. Mercury, Earth, Venus, Mars  
D. Venus, Mercury, Earth, Mars  

### Intervention Hints:
- Level 1: Start with the very first one from the Sun – which is closest?
- Level 2: Use your memory or recall from the order: Sun → Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars...
- Level 3: Visualize where these planets are located if you lined them up with the Sun.

✅ Correct answer: A. Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars

What causes the seasons on Earth?

A. The tilt of Earth's axis  
B. Earth's distance from the Sun  
C. The Moon's gravity  
D. The Sun's temperature  

### MC Choices:
A. The tilt of Earth's axis  
B. Earth's distance from the Sun  
C. The Moon's gravity  
D. The Sun's temperature  

### Intervention Hints:
- Level 1: What happens to daylight hours over the year in Canada?
- Level 2: When it's summer in Canada, which hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun?

## Stage 5: Capstone
### Problem:
A student from Montreal wants to know how the Moon affects ocean tides on Earth. You explain that:
1. The gravitational pull from the Moon causes ocean water to bulge.
2. This creates high tides and low tides.
3. The Moon's gravity also pulls the land slightly, which also affects tides.

Which of the following is the most accurate statement?

A. The Moon doesn’t affect Earth's tides  
B. Earth's rotation causes the tides  
C. Tides depend on the Moon and Earth's position  
D. Only the Sun causes tides  

### MC Choices:
A. The Moon doesn’t affect Earth's tides  
B. Earth's rotation causes the tides  
C. Tides depend on the Moon and Earth's position  
D. Only the Sun causes tides  

### Intervention Hints:
- Level 1: Which force pulls Earth’s water in different directions?
- Level 2: Does it matter where the Moon is in its orbit?
- Level 3: What other celestial object influences the sea levels?

✅ Correct answer: C. Tides depend on the Moon and Earth's position
