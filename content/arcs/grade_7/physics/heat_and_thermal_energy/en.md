## Stage 1: Hook

### Hook Story – “The Ice Cube Race”

Imagine it's a hot summer day in Vancouver and two friends, Maya and Alex, are having a fun challenge. They each have a cup of hot tea. The cups are identical, and both teas are the same temperature.

Maya's cup is placed on a metal table. Alex's cup is on a wooden table.

After five minutes, they check their tea.

Maya's tea is cooler, and Alex's tea is still hot.

Why did this happen?

> The idea of "temperature" and "thermal energy" are both related to heat, but they are different things!
> 
> The hotter the tea, the more thermal energy it has.

### Hook Story – “Winter’s Warmth”

In a small town in Ontario, it's a snowy winter day and Sarah goes outside to fetch her mail. She has on thick gloves and notices that it feels quite cold outside.

As she holds her mittens, they quickly warm up, but Sarah's fingers stay cold.

She notices that:
- The mittens warm up because they're good *thermal insulators*.
- Her fingers stay cold because they're good *thermal conductors*.

## Stage 2: Concept

### Concept

We will learn about heat, temperature, and thermal energy.

1. **Temperature** – how hot or cold something is (measured with a thermometer).
2. **Thermal Energy** – the total energy of all the particles (tiny invisible pieces) of matter. The more particles move back and forth, the more thermal energy.

> Example: A cup of hot coffee has a high temperature, but a large swimming pool filled with warm water has more thermal energy than the coffee.

3. **Heat Transfer** – Heat always moves from a hot object to a cold object until both are at the same temperature.
   - **Conduction**: Heat moves through direct contact (like when you touch a hot pan).
   - **Convection**: Heat moves through fluids (liquids or gases) as currents (like hot air rising).
   - **Radiation**: Heat moves through empty space (like sunlight warming the earth).

4. **Conductors and Insulators**:
   - **Conductors** – let heat move through them easily. (Examples: metal spoon, copper wires)
   - **Insulators** – don’t let heat move through them easily. (Examples: wood, ceramic, plastic)

5. **Thermal Expansion** – when materials are heated, they usually expand or get bigger. When cooled, they contract or get smaller.

> Example: Railway tracks expand in hot weather to prevent buckling.

### Comprehension Check

**Question:**

Which statement correctly explains the difference between temperature and thermal energy?

A. Temperature measures how fast molecules are moving; thermal energy measures the total energy of molecules.

B. Temperature is the same as thermal energy; they are just different words for the same thing.

C. Thermal energy measures how fast the molecules are moving; temperature is how much heat is in a system.

D. Temperature measures how hot something is; thermal energy is the total amount of heat in an object.

**Correct Answer:** A

**Interventions:**

- **Level 1:** "Think about a pot of hot soup and a large swimming pool. Both are hot, but which has more energy?"
- **Level 2:** "Imagine shaking a ball very fast versus shaking it slowly — how does that relate to molecules moving in a hot object compared to a cold one?"
- **Level 3:** "In a classroom with 20 students, one is very energetic, but the class is calm. Which is more like temperature? Which is more like thermal energy?"

## Stage 3: Guided

### Guided Problem:

**Question:**

In a science experiment, you place a metal spoon in a cup of hot tea. After a few minutes, you touch the handle of the spoon. Why does the handle become warm?

A. The tea directly heats the spoon.
B. Heat is transferred through the spoon by conduction.
C. The heat from the tea moves through the air.
D. The metal spoon has thermal energy.

**Correct Answer:** B

**Interventions:**

- **Level 1:** "When you touch the spoon, where does the heat come from? Does it come from the hot air, or from the spoon itself?"
- **Level 2:** "Think about what happens in a race. What helps the baton pass quickly from one runner to the next? What is that like for heat moving through the spoon?"
- **Level 3:** "In heat transfer, what happens when you have a warm object and a cool one in contact? Does heat move like a wave or like something that touches?"

## Stage 4: Practice

### Practice Problems

#### Problem A:

**Question:**

Which of the following examples shows a situation where convection is happening?

A. A metal fork placed in hot soup gets warm.
B. Warm air from a heater rises to the ceiling.
C. Sunlight heats up a black car seat.
D. Hot coffee cools down in a ceramic mug.

**Correct Answer:** B

**Interventions:**

- **Level 1:** "Look at a heated room. What happens to the hot air? Where does it go?"
- **Level 2:** "Imagine hot soup on a stove that’s bubbling. The bubbles rise. That’s convection in liquids!"
- **Level 3:** "What does 'convection' mean? 'Convect' means to carry or move. So heat is carried by movement of air."

#### Problem B:

**Question:**

Which of these is an example of a thermal insulator?

A. Steel door handle
B. Wooden cutting board
C. Aluminum pot
D. Copper wire

**Correct Answer:** B

**Interventions:**

- **Level 1:** "Think about touching hot things. Do you feel better with a wooden spoon or a metal spoon when you're cooking?"
- **Level 2:** "What do you think happens when you put ice on a metal tray? Does it melt fast or slow? What about on a wooden tray?"
- **Level 3:** "An insulator blocks or slows the flow of heat. Which one would slow heat the most — a metal or a wood?"

#### Problem C:

**Question:**

Which of the following best describes the process of thermal expansion?

A. Water expands when it turns to ice.
B. A steel bridge expands in summer heat.
C. A cup breaks when heated too quickly.
D. Hot air rises and cold air sinks.

**Correct Answer:** B

**Interventions:**

- **Level 1:** "Think about a bridge in summer. It gets longer and wider because of heat."
- **Level 2:** "What happens to rubber bands when you heat them up? They stretch!"
- **Level 3:** "If a train track gets longer, and it gets longer in heat, that’s expanding. And where do trains move fast in summer?"

## Stage 5: Capstone

### Capstone Problem:

**Question:**

A science teacher wants to demonstrate how heat moves through different materials using a metal rod and a wooden rod. At one end of each rod, a candle is held, while at the other end, a piece of wax is attached. The candle heats one end and melts the wax on the opposite side.

Which rod will melt the wax more quickly if both are the same size and heat source?

A. The wooden rod, because it allows heat to travel slowly.
B. The metal rod, because it is a better thermal conductor.
C. Both rods will melt the wax at the same rate.
D. It depends on the type of wax used.

**Correct Answer:** B

**Interventions (between attempt 1 and 2 only):**

> “The metal rod conducts heat more easily than the wooden rod. If heat travels quickly, which will melt wax faster?”

If passed:
> "Excellent! You've used your knowledge of conductors and heat transfer to understand what happens in real science. This is how thermometers are made, how stoves work, and how we keep our homes warm."

If failed after 2 attempts:
> "Heat transfer can be tricky and involves understanding conductors and how easily heat moves through them. This is a deeper concept and is important when designing things like buildings or car engines. Let's come back to it next time!"

#### Alternate Capstone:

**Question:**

Which of these best explains why a home's basement stays cooler in the summer than the upstairs?

A. The basement has more insulation.
B. Cool air sinks, so it settles in the basement.
C. The basement is smaller, so it gets less sun.
D. Water in the basement cools the air.

**Correct Answer:** B

**Interventions (between attempt 1 and 2 only):**

> “Warm air rises. Which part of the house would be warmer – the basement or the upstairs? Why does that happen?”

### Real World Application Questions:

#### Real-World Example A:

**Question:**

In a cold winter day, why should you wear a jacket with thick insulation inside?

A. The insulation increases your body temperature.
B. The insulation prevents heat from escaping your body.
C. The jacket reflects the sun’s heat.
D. The jacket is heavy, so it stops heat from escaping.

**Correct Answer:** B

#### Real-World Example B:

**Question:**

Why does a car dashboard get hot in summer?

A. It has many insulators.
B. It is made of a material that conducts heat.
C. It is exposed to sunlight.
D. It heats up only from the engine.

**Correct Answer:** B

## Stage 6: Wrap-Up

### Summary:

We learned that:

- **Temperature** is how hot or cold something is.
- **Thermal Energy** is the total amount of energy in a substance due to movement of its particles.
- **Heat Transfer** happens in three ways:
  - **Conduction** – heat moves by direct contact.
  - **Convection** – heat moves through fluids like air or water.
  - **Radiation** – heat moves through space.
- **Conductors** (like metal) help heat move easily.
- **Insulators** (like wood or plastic) slow heat transfer.
- **Thermal Expansion** – materials get bigger when heated.

You now know how heat moves and how we use this knowledge in everyday life – from building homes to making cars, from cooking to keeping warm.

## Stage 7: Assessment – Optional Extension

### Assessment – “Design a Safe Space”

Students can work in teams to design a safe space for an outdoor event on a hot summer day. The design should consider:

- Materials used in the roof and walls that keep the inside cool (insulators)
- How to prevent heat from entering the space (radiation shielding)
- The effect of air moving in and out (convection)
- Why some materials should or shouldn't be used (conductors vs. insulators)

**Extensions / Activities:**

- Create a small model of a house using insulating materials vs. conducting materials.
- Try a real classroom demonstration (e.g., melting wax on rods).
- Use a thermostat to show temperature differences in different rooms.
