## Stage 1: Hook — pH in Everyday Life

### Hook Story 1: The Sour Lemon and the Slippery Soap

Imagine you're doing a science project on household items. You take a lemon and squeeze some juice onto a small piece of paper. Then, you take a bar of soap and rub it on the same piece of paper.

You notice the lemon juice makes your fingers feel sour, like you just bit into a lemon. The soap, on the other hand, feels slippery against your skin.

This is because the lemon juice is acidic and the soap is basic. These are special types of substances we call acids and bases.

In science, we can measure how acidic or basic a substance is using a thing called the pH scale. The pH scale is marked from 0 to 14, and it tells us how "acidic" or "basic" something is.

### Hook Story 2: The Canada Weather and Rain

In Canada, especially in areas like Ontario and Quebec, we sometimes hear about acid rain. Acid rain is rain that is more acidic than normal.

Imagine you are outside during a heavy rainstorm. The water from the sky feels a bit strange — it doesn't have the usual clean taste or feel of fresh water. This is because there are chemicals in the air that mix with the rainwater, making it acidic.

This kind of rain can hurt plants, animals, and even buildings!

Let's look at the pH scale to understand how we measure acidity, and how acids and bases work in our daily lives.

## Stage 2: Concept — pH Scale and Acids vs. Bases

### Concept Explanation:

**The pH Scale is used to measure how acidic or basic a solution is.**

- A **pH of 0–6** = **Acid** – It is sour, and can be corrosive.
- A **pH of 7** = **Neutral** – It is neither acidic nor basic.
- A **pH of 8–14** = **Base** – It is bitter or slippery.

**Indicators** are substances that change color in response to pH. Some common indicators:

- **Litmus paper** – It turns red in acids and blue in bases.
- **pH paper** – It changes to many colors depending on the pH.
- **Red cabbage juice** – It turns pink in acids, green in bases, and purple in neutral substances.

**Neutralization** is a reaction that happens when an acid and a base mix together and cancel each other out.

**Real-world examples** of acids and bases:

| Substance       | Type      | pH        |
|----------------|-----------|-----------|
| Stomach acid   | Acid      | 1.5–2     |
| Baking soda    | Base      | 8–9       |
| Vinegar        | Acid      | 3         |
| Pool water     | Base      | 7.2–7.8   |
| Rainwater      | Acid      | 5.6       |
| Soap           | Base      | 9–10      |

### Concept Comprehension Check:

**Which of the following is NOT an example of an acid?**

A. Lemon juice  
B. Baking soda  
C. Vinegar  
D. Stomach acid  

**Correct answer:** B. Baking soda  
**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** “Think about which of these are sour or corrosive.”
- **Level 2:** “Lemon juice, vinegar, and stomach acid are all acids. What about baking soda?”
- **Level 3:** “We learned that baking soda is basic because it's slippery and bitter. This is what makes it useful for cleaning.”

## Stage 3: Guided Problem — Testing pH with Indicators

### Guided Problem:

Sarah is doing a science experiment. She has four test tubes with different solutions.

- Solution A: Turns red litmus paper red.
- Solution B: Turns blue litmus paper blue.
- Solution C: Turns red cabbage juice green.
- Solution D: Turns red cabbage juice pink.

Which solution is most likely to be a base?

**A. Solution A**  
**B. Solution B**  
**C. Solution C**  
**D. Solution D**

**Correct answer:** B. Solution B

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** “Let’s think, which indicator tells us if something is a base?”
- **Level 2:** “Red litmus paper turns blue when it's in a base. Blue litmus paper turns red in acids.”
- **Level 3:** “We know that litmus paper helps us test acids and bases, and solution B makes blue litmus stay blue — that means it's a base!”

## Stage 4: Practice — pH and Mixtures

### Problem 1:

What happens when an acid and a base are mixed together?

A. The mixture becomes more acidic  
B. The mixture becomes more basic  
C. The mixture becomes neutral  
D. The mixture becomes hotter  

**Correct answer:** C. The mixture becomes neutral

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** “Remember what we call mixing acids and bases.”
- **Level 2:** “The acid and base react to make something new. It isn’t acid or base anymore.”
- **Level 3:** “That’s called neutralization — the acid and base “cancel out” and the result is usually neutral.”

### Problem 2:

Which of the following is a real-world example of a neutral substance?

A. Lemon juice  
B. Bleach  
C. Pure water  
D. Vinegar  

**Correct answer:** C. Pure water

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** “Think about what pH number corresponds to neutral.”
- **Level 2:** “We know water is neutral with a pH of 7. So it’s not acidic or basic.”
- **Level 3:** “Lemon juice is acidic (pH 2). Vinegar is acidic too. Bleach is basic. Only pure water is exactly neutral.”

### Problem 3:

What color does red cabbage juice turn in a basic solution?

A. Pink  
B. Green  
C. Purple  
D. Yellow  

**Correct answer:** B. Green

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** “Red cabbage juice gives different colors based on pH. What happens in a base?”
- **Level 2:** “We told you that in a base, red cabbage juice turns green, because that’s how it reacts.”
- **Level 3:** “If it's an acid, it goes pink. If it's basic, it goes green. If it's neutral, it's purple.”

## Stage 5: Capstone — Real-Life pH Problems

### Capstone Problem:

**A science student is testing several common household substances to find their pH.**

1. **Stomach acid** – What type of substance is it?
   - A. Acid
   - B. Base
   - C. Neutral
   - D. Salt

2. **Household cleaning product** – It feels slippery and is used to clean toilets. What type is it?
   - A. Acid
   - B. Base
   - C. Neutral
   - D. Gas

3. **A person drinks vinegar for health benefits** – What effect does it have?
   - A. Makes the body more acidic
   - B. Makes the body more basic
   - C. Does not change the body’s pH
   - D. Makes the body neutral

**Correct answers:**
1. A. Acid  
2. B. Base  
3. A. Makes the body more acidic
