# Stage 1: Hook

**Hook Story:**

Sarah is shopping online for a new backpack. She finds two deals:

- Deal A: Buy 2 backpacks for \$40
- Deal B: Buy 3 backpacks for \$60

She wants to know which deal offers the better price per backpack. 

Which deal has the lower price per backpack?

A) Deal A: \$20 per backpack  
B) Deal B: \$20 per backpack  
C) Both deals are the same price per backpack  
D) It depends on the brand of backpack

**Alternative Hook Story:**

The Canadian government is planning a recycling program. In one neighborhood, 50 people recycled 200 kg of materials in one week. In another neighborhood, 30 people recycled 120 kg in a week.

Which neighborhood had a **higher** average amount of items recycled **per person**?

A) The first neighborhood  
B) The second neighborhood  
C) Both neighborhoods are the same  
D) Not enough information

**Narrative:** Proportional reasoning helps us understand how things relate to each other. Whether we're comparing prices or seeing how much someone recycled, this skill helps us make smart decisions!

# Stage 2: Concept

**Concept Summary:**
Proportional relationships are when two quantities change together at a constant rate. We can figure out how much one quantity is for one unit of another — this is called the **unit rate** or **constant of proportionality**.

**Examples:**
- If a recipe needs 2 cups of flour for every 1 cup of sugar, the ratio is 2:1.
- For every 1 kg of apples, the price is \$3.00. This is a unit rate of $3/kg.

**How to Tell If It's Proportional:**
- All ratios are the same (like 2:1, 4:2, 6:3)
- Graphs form a straight line through the origin (0,0)
- Equations are in the form `y = kx`, where k is the constant of proportionality

**Comprehension Check:**

Which table shows a proportional relationship?

| A | B |  
|---|---|  
| x | y |  
| 1 | 5 |  
| 2 | 10 |  
| 3 | 15 |  

| C | D |  
|---|---|  
| x | y |  
| 1 | 5 |  
| 2 | 10 |  
| 3 | 12 |  

A) Table A  
B) Table B  
C) Both A and B  
D) Neither A nor B  

**Interventions:**

- **Level 1:** Look at the values. What happens to y when x doubles?  
- **Level 2:** Try dividing each y by its matching x (y/x). If they are all the same, it’s proportional.  
- **Level 3:** Does the graph of this relationship go through (0,0)? If not, it’s not proportional.

# Stage 4: Practice

**Problem 1: Percent of a Number**

What is 25% of 80?

A) 15  
B) 20  
C) 40  
D) 65  

**Interventions:**  
- **Level 1:** Do 25% of the whole number  
- **Level 2:** Turn 25% into a fraction or decimal (25/100 = 1/4)  
- **Level 3:** Think 25% of 80 means 1/4 of 80 = 20  

---

**Problem 2: Percent Increase/Decrease** 

The price of a bicycle went from \$200 to \$240. What was the percent increase?

A) 10%  
B) 15%  
C) 20%  
D) 25%  

**Interventions:**  
- **Level 1:** Find the difference in price. Then how much is that of original price?  
- **Level 2:** Price increased by \$40. What percent is \$40 of \$200?  
- **Level 3:** Think: 40 ÷ 200 = 0.2 → 20%  

---

**Problem 3: Tax and Tip**

At a restaurant, the bill is \$45. The tax rate is 8%. The customer gives a 15% tip. 

How much was the tip?

A) \$6.75  
B) \$3.60  
C) \$8.35  
D) \$5.40  

**Interventions:**  
- **Level 1:** Tax and tip are usually on the bill before tax  
- **Level 2:** Tip is 15% of \$45  
- **Level 3:** 15% of 45 = 0.15 × 45 = 6.75

# Stage 5: Capstone

**Problem:**

A basketball player scored 20 baskets in 5 games. 

If he continues scoring at the same rate, how many baskets will he score in 12 games?

1. **How many baskets per game?**
2. **How many baskets in 12 games?**

A) 4 baskets per game; 48 baskets in 12 games  
B) 4 baskets per game; 60 baskets in 12 games  
C) 5 baskets per game; 60 baskets in 12 games  
D) 5 baskets per game; 48 baskets in 12 games  

---

**Guidance Between First and Second Attempt:**

"First, work out how many baskets he scores in one game. Then you can use that to find how many he scores in 12 games."

**If Passed:**

"You just solved a real-world proportional reasoning problem — using rate, unit rate, and extension to find the answer. You’re really building your math skills!"

**If Failed After 2 Attempts:**

"This one had two steps — finding the rate, then applying it. That’s a bit hard but you’re making progress. We’ll work on it more next time!"
