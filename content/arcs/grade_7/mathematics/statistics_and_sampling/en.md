## Stage 1: Hook
**Topic:** Statistics and Sampling  
**Subtopic:** Introduction to sampling and inference  
**Focus:** Why sample data instead of surveying entire populations  
**Language:** en  

In a busy Canadian city like Toronto, the city council wants to know what people think of a new public transit line. They can't ask every single person in the city. Instead, they collect opinions from a small group of people who represent the whole city’s population — this is called sampling.

Imagine you are a grade 7 student working on a science project about healthy snacks for school. Your teacher asks you to find out what snacks your classmates like best. You don’t have time to ask everyone in your school — so you ask just a sample of students from different grades. What you find out about the sample might help you understand what most of your school thinks.

Why is this useful? Because it's much easier and cheaper than asking everyone, and it still gives a good idea of what the whole group thinks. The results from a good sample are more accurate than a guess — they give valid inferences about the entire population.

Now, let's say a sports company wants to know which basketball shoe style is most popular with students. If they only ask students from one school, that might not represent all Canadian students. But if they ask several students from many different schools, they'll get a better idea. This is why using good sampling techniques is so important!

## Stage 2: Concept
**Topic:** Statistics and Sampling  
**Subtopic:** Random Sampling  
**Focus:** What makes a sample random, and why random sampling is best for inference  
**Language:** en  

### Learning Goals:
- Understand that a sample should be representative of the population  
- Define random sampling and explain its importance  
- Explain how random sampling leads to more valid inferences  
- Contrast random with biased sampling  

### Comprehension Check:
**Question:**  
Why is it important to use random sampling when collecting data?

- A. It makes the data easy to understand  
- B. It makes sure that every person in the population has an equal chance to be selected  
- C. It helps you collect more data quickly  
- D. It allows you to ignore outliers  

### Interventions:
**Level 1:** "Think — if you are going to draw a random sample, what does that mean for your method?"  
**Level 2:** "When everyone in the group has the same chance to be chosen, is it more likely to reflect what the whole group thinks or just one opinion?"  
**Level 3:** "Is the selection method fair to everyone? Does every individual have an equal chance of being in your sample?"

## Stage 3: Guided
**Topic:** Statistics and Sampling  
**Subtopic:** Identifying Valid Inferences  
**Focus:** Making valid claims based on sample data  
**Language:** en  

### Problem:
In a survey about school lunch preferences, 200 students were randomly selected from a school of 1,000 students. Out of the 200, 40 said they liked pizza most. Which of these is the most valid inference based on this sample?

- A. Most students at this school like pizza  
- B. Exactly 40 students in the whole school like pizza  
- C. Half of the students in the school like pizza  
- D. All of the students at the school like pizza  

### Interventions:
**Level 1:** "How many students were surveyed out of how many in the school? Could you make a guess?"  
**Level 2:** "If 40 out of 200 liked pizza, what fraction does that represent? Does it prove that the whole school likes pizza?"  
**Level 3:** "What does 'most students' mean? Could you use this sample to decide what the entire school thinks?"

## Stage 4: Practice
**Topic:** Statistics and Sampling  
**Subtopic:** Comparing Data Sets Using Measures of Centre and Spread  
**Focus:** Using mean, median, range, IQR, MAD  
**Language:** en  

### Problem 1:
Two basketball teams played a series of games. Team A had scores of 78, 82, 75, 80, 85. Team B had scores of 70, 85, 80, 90, 75. Which team had a higher median score?

- A. Team A  
- B. Team B  
- C. Both teams had the same median  
- D. It's impossible to tell  

### Interventions:
**Level 1:** "Sort each team's scores from least to greatest. Which number is in the middle?"  
**Level 2:** "What’s the middle of 75, 78, 80, 82, 85?"  
**Level 3:** "For each team, find the exact middle score after sorting."

### Problem 2:
A survey was done to measure the time students spend on homework. Team X has a range of 45 minutes, and Team Y has a range of 60 minutes. What does that tell us about the data?

- A. Team Y has more students  
- B. Team X students spend more time on homework  
- C. Team Y’s students’ homework times vary more  
- D. It's impossible to know without mean values  

### Interventions:
**Level 1:** "What does range mean? Can it tell us about variation, not just time?"  
**Level 2:** "If one team has a higher range, what does that suggest about their homework times?"  
**Level 3:** "Is it better when data is spread out or when it is clustered?"

### Problem 3:
Sarah recorded the number of hours she studied each day for a week: 2, 3, 5, 4, 3, 6, 4. What is the mean absolute deviation (MAD) of her study hours?

- A. 1  
- B. 1.2  
- C. 1.5  
- D. 2  

### Interventions:
**Level 1:** "Calculate the mean first. Then find the difference between each value and the mean."  
**Level 2:** "Subtract the mean from each value, take absolute value, and find the mean of those absolute differences."  
**Level 3:** "If you know each step, you’ll need to compute mean and average difference accurately."

## Stage 5: Capstone
**Topic:** Statistics and Sampling  
**Subtopic:** Drawing Inferences from Box-and-Whisker Plots and Comparing Data Sets  
**Focus:** Real-world data interpretation using box plots and descriptive statistics  
**Language:** en  

### Problem:
A researcher compared the weights of two types of penguins: Emperor and Adelie. The box-and-whisker plots show the data. The Emperor penguin's median weight is 42 kg, and the Adelie penguin's median is 35 kg. The IQR of Emperor penguins is 6 kg, and that of Adelie penguins is 8 kg.

Which conclusion is BEST supported by the data?

- A. Emperor penguins are heavier, and their weights vary less  
- B. Emperor penguins are heavier, and their weights vary more  
- C. Adelie penguins are heavier, and their weights vary less  
- D. Adelie penguins are heavier, and their weights vary more  

### Interventions:
**Level 1:** "Which penguin has the higher median weight? What does a wider IQR show?"  
**Level 2:** "The median tells us average weight. The IQR shows how spread out the data is. Are the Emperor penguins heavier and more consistent or less consistent?"  
**Level 3:** "If one median is higher and one IQR is smaller, what can you conclude about both weight and consistency?"
