## Stage 2: Concept — Probability of Compound Events

**Concept:**
Compound probability involves multiple independent or dependent events and can be found by multiplying the probabilities of each event. It answers questions like: What are the chances of flipping a coin twice and getting two tails? Or getting a red card and a heart from a deck?

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand that independent events don’t affect each other.
- Learn that the probability of two independent events both happening is the product of their individual probabilities.
- Compare independent to dependent events.
- Use tree diagrams, lists, or the Fundamental Counting Principle to find total outcomes.
- Recognize how theoretical probability differs from experimental probability.

**Comprehension Check:**
**Problem:**
A student picks two cards at random without replacement from a standard deck of 52 cards.

How to determine the probability of picking a red card first and then a black card?

**MC Choices:**
1.  $\frac{26}{52} \times \frac{26}{51}$  
2.  $\frac{26}{52} \times \frac{26}{52}$  
3.  $\frac{26}{52} + \frac{26}{51}$  
4.  $\frac{26}{52} \times \frac{25}{51}$  

**Intervention Hints:**
- **Level 1:** “Does the chance of drawing a black card change if a red card has already been picked?”
- **Level 2:** “Think about whether the card is put back or not. What does that mean for your calculations?”
- **Level 3:** “The probabilities depend on the remaining number of cards. After drawing one, how many cards are left?”

**Correct Answer:** 1. $\frac{26}{52} \times \frac{26}{51}$

## Stage 3: Guided — Compound Probability Problems

**Problem:**
You spin a spinner with 4 equal sections (red, blue, green, yellow) and flip a coin. What is the probability of spinning red and flipping heads?

**MC Choices:**
1.  $\frac{1}{4}$  
2.  $\frac{1}{8}$  
3.  $\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{2}$  
4.  $\frac{1}{4} \times \frac{1}{2}$  

**Intervention Hints:**
- **Level 1:** "Are the spinner and coin flips affecting each other? Can the result of one change the result of the other?"
- **Level 2:** "These events are independent. How do we calculate the probability of two independent events occurring together?"
- **Level 3:** "Independent events are calculated by multiplying the probability of each event. What fraction is the chance of landing on red? What’s the chance of flipping heads?"

**Correct Answer:** 4. $\frac{1}{4} \times \frac{1}{2}$

## Stage 4: Practice — Applying Compound Probability

**Problem 1:**
In a deck of cards, what is the chance of drawing a king, replacing it, then drawing a heart?

**MC Choices:**
1.  $\frac{4}{52} + \frac{13}{52}$  
2.  $\frac{4}{52} \times \frac{13}{52}$  
3.  $\frac{4}{52}$  
4.  $\frac{1}{52} \times \frac{1}{52}$  

**Intervention Hints:**
- **Level 1:** "Is the card put back in the deck after being drawn? What effect does that have?"
- **Level 2:** "Since the card is replaced, does the outcome change the odds?"
- **Level 3:** "When there's replacement, probabilities stay the same for each draw. What’s the chance of getting a king first? What’s the chance of a heart after replacing?"

**Correct Answer:** 2. $\frac{4}{52} \times \frac{13}{52}$

---

**Problem 2:**
A die and a coin are used in a game. What’s the probability of rolling a 6 and getting tails?

**MC Choices:**
1.  $\frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{2}$  
2.  $\frac{1}{12}$  
3.  $\frac{1}{6} \times \frac{1}{2}$  
4.  $\frac{1}{6} \times \frac{1}{6}$  

**Intervention Hints:**
- **Level 1:** "Is rolling a dice affected by flipping a coin or vice versa?"
- **Level 2:** "These are separate actions that do not influence each other’s outcome – what do we call this?"
- **Level 3:** "For independent events, we multiply probability. What is the chance of the die showing six? What is the chance of coins showing tails?"

**Correct Answer:** 3. $\frac{1}{6} \times \frac{1}{2}$

---

**Problem 3:**
You are picking a marble from a bag and not replacing it, then picking another marble. What is the probability of picking a red marble first and a blue marble second?

**MC Choices:**
1.  $\frac{4}{10} \times \frac{3}{9}$  
2.  $\frac{4}{10} \times \frac{4}{9}$  
3.  $\frac{4}{10} + \frac{3}{9}$  
4.  $\frac{4}{10} \times \frac{1}{10}$  

**Intervention Hints:**
- **Level 1:** "What happens to the number of marbles in the bag after one is picked and not replaced?"
- **Level 2:** "The number of total marbles changes — does this change the probability of the next draw?"
- **Level 3:** "After removing one marble, the denominator changes. What’s the chance of picking a red marble? What changes after that?"

**Correct Answer:** 1. $\frac{4}{10} \times \frac{3}{9}$

## Stage 5: Capstone — Real World Applications

**Problem:**
A school board game tournament has two rounds.

1. Round One: Flip a coin and roll a die. If you get tails and a number greater than 3, you move to Round Two.
2. Round Two: Pick a card from a deck. If it’s a heart, you win a prize.

What is the probability of winning a prize?

**Two parts:**
1. What’s the probability of reaching Round Two?
2. What’s the final probability of winning the prize?

**MC Choices for Part 1:**
1.  $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{3}{6}$  
2.  $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{3}{6}$  
3.  $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{3}{5}$  
4.  $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{6}$  

**MC Choices for Part 2:**
1.  $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{3}{6} \times \frac{13}{52}$  
2.  $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{13}{52}$  
3.  $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{3}{6} \times \frac{1}{52}$  
4.  $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{3}{6} + \frac{13}{52}$  

**Intervention Hints:**
- **Level 1:** "Think of how to find the probability of one event. Multiply or add?"
- **Level 2:** "Round Two has a card draw — what’s the chance of drawing a heart from a full deck?"
- **Level 3:** "Remember that probabilities are multiplied in independent events. Find the probability of reaching Round Two, then the chance of winning.”

**Correct Answers:**
- **Part 1:** 1.  $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{3}{6}$  
- **Part 2:** 1.  $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{3}{6} \times \frac{13}{52}$

## Stage 6: Recap — Understanding the Concepts

**Recap Reflection:**
This arc helped students understand that compound probability involves calculating events that happen together. When events are independent (such as spinning a spinner and flipping a coin), we multiply their probabilities. When there is no replacement, the probability of the second event changes because the total number of outcomes changes.

**Final Recap Questions:**
1. When are events independent?
2. How do probabilities change when items are not replaced?
3. What method helps to list all possible outcomes?

**Answers:**
1. Independent events do not affect each other.
2. The probability changes because the number of total outcomes decreases.
3. Tree diagrams, lists, and the Fundamental Counting Principle.
