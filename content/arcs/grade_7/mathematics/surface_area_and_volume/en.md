## Stage 1: Hook
### Title: What's the Size of the Box?
**Story:** Emma is packing a gift box for her grandmother. The box is shaped like a rectangular prism, and she wants to wrap it in beautiful wrapping paper. The box measures 10 cm long, 6 cm wide, and 4 cm high. Emma wants to know how much wrapping paper she needs to cover the entire box. 

How much paper should Emma buy?

- A) 160 cm²
- B) 200 cm²
- C) 240 cm²
- D) 280 cm²

Emma's mom says: “If you were building that box out of cardboard, how many square centimeters of cardboard would you need?” 

This question is about **surface area** – how much **surface** or area you need to cover the **outside** of the object.

## Stage 2: Concept
### Title: The Surface Area of a Rectangular Prism

#### Learning Objective:
Understand how to find the surface area of a rectangular prism by unfolding it into a net and adding up all faces.

#### Concept:
A rectangular prism has 6 faces: 2 length × width, 2 length × height, and 2 width × height. To find **total surface area**, we calculate:
- Area of 2 length × width faces
- Area of 2 length × height faces  
- Area of 2 width × height faces
- Then add them all together

#### Net Example:
```
     [ 6 cm ]
   _____________
  |             |
  |             |  4 cm
  |             |
  |_____________|
     [ 10 cm ]
```
Each of the 6 rectangles is unfolded for calculation.

#### Interventions:
- Level 1: “How many faces does a rectangular prism have?”
- Level 2: “Which faces are the same size? Think length × width, etc.”
- Level 3: “If the front is 6 × 4, what is the back equal to?”
  

#### Comprehension Check:
What is the surface area of a rectangular prism that is 8 cm long, 5 cm wide, and 3 cm high?

- A) 120 cm²
- B) 150 cm²
- C) 170 cm²
- D) 190 cm²

## Stage 3: Guided
### Title: Let's Find the Surface Area

#### Problem:
A gift box is shaped like a rectangular prism with dimensions 12 cm × 8 cm × 5 cm. How much wrapping paper does the gift box need to cover the full surface?

#### Method:
1. Identify all faces:
   - 2 faces: 12 cm × 8 cm
   - 2 faces: 12 cm × 5 cm
   - 2 faces: 8 cm × 5 cm
2. Multiply each face’s area:
   - 12 × 8 = 96 → 2 × 96 = 192 cm²
   - 12 × 5 = 60 → 2 × 60 = 120 cm²
   - 8 × 5 = 40 → 2 × 40 = 80 cm²
3. Total surface area = 192 + 120 + 80 = **392 cm²**

#### Interventions:
- Level 1: “Which dimensions are paired together?”
- Level 2: “Are all faces the same? How many are the same?”
- Level 3: “If the 12 × 8 faces total 192 cm², what about the 12 × 5 faces?”  

#### Multiple Choice:
What is the surface area of the box above?

- A) 320 cm²
- B) 340 cm²
- C) 370 cm²
- D) 392 cm²

### Title: The Cylindrical Tank

#### Problem 2:
A cylindrical water tank has a radius of 4 cm and a height of 10 cm. What is the surface area of the tank?

- A) 351.68 cm²
- B) 401.92 cm²
- C) 502.4 cm²
- D) 602.88 cm²

#### Interventions:
- Level 1: “What’s the difference between a cylinder's total surface area and just its curved surface?”
- Level 2: “What do you need to find for each of the two circular top and bottom faces?”

## Stage 5: Capstone

### Title: Designing the Perfect Box

#### Problem:
You are designing a packaging box for a Canadian-made candle company. The box is a rectangular prism with dimensions 10 cm × 5 cm × 3 cm. You want to know:

1. How much **wrapping paper** (surface area) will be needed?
2. How much **candle wax** (volume) the box can hold.

What are your answers?

#### Calculation 1:
Surface area:
- 2 × (10 × 5) = 100 cm²
- 2 × (10 × 3) = 60 cm²  
- 2 × (5 × 3) = 30 cm²
- Total = 100 + 60 + 30 = **190 cm²**

#### Calculation 2:
Volume: 10 × 5 × 3 = **150 cm³**

#### Multiple Choice:
Which is the correct answer for both parts?

- A) 170 cm² and 120 cm³
- B) 190 cm² and 150 cm³
- C) 210 cm² and 180 cm³
- D) 230 cm² and 200 cm³

#### Interventions (if needed):
- Level 1: “What shapes do you see on the box? Which formulas do you need?”
- Level 2: “Which faces are identical? What’s the area of those?”
- Level 3: “Did you double-check both surface area and volume?”

#### Guiding Question (between attempts):
> "Start by listing what you know: dimensions and type of shape. Then identify which calculations are needed."
