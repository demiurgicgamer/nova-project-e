## Stage 1: Hook

**Hook Story:** *The Arctic Adventure*

Alex is a young explorer participating in a research expedition in the Canadian Arctic. The team has set up camp, and as part of their daily routine, they monitor temperature changes for a science project. On one particularly cold morning, the temperature read -3.5°C, and by midday, it rose to 4.2°C. 

"Wait," said Alex's teammate, Sarah, who's studying weather patterns. "We went from negative to positive! That’s like a temperature flip!"

Alex thought for a second and smiled. "Well, it's not a flip, it's a change. We had to add what we gained to the original number." 

Sarah looked confused. "So, what was the overall change?"

Can you help Alex explain to Sarah the math behind this temperature change, using both fractions and decimals?

**Hook Outcome:**
This hook sets the stage for students to encounter rational numbers in a relatable, Canadian context. As we explore more about the Arctic, temperature becomes a key element for understanding how positive and negative numbers behave when added or subtracted.

## Stage 2: Concept

**Conceptual Overview:**

In this stage, students will be introduced to positive and negative fractions and decimals, and how they relate to real-world contexts. We'll learn to perform the four basic operations – addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division – with these numbers.

We'll also explore what happens to numbers when they are placed between brackets, what the absolute value of a number means, and how to apply the order of operations with these types of numbers.

**Comprehension Check:**  
What does it mean to subtract a negative number?

**A) You add it**  
**B) You multiply it**  
**C) You take the absolute value**  
**D) It's the same as adding the positive number**

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** You can think of this like "taking away a debt" — so if you owe 5 dollars and someone takes away that debt, you are gaining 5 dollars.
- **Level 2:** Subtracting a negative is like going in the opposite direction. Think of a number line. If you go backward from -8 by 5, you're actually going forward towards zero.
- **Level 3:** Let's think of it as: 8 - (-5) = 8 + 5. That's because subtracting a negative number is the same mathematically as adding the positive value.

## Stage 3: Guided

**Guided Problem:** *Mystery Numbers in Finance*

Maria, a young financial intern, is tracking the performance of her company’s investment portfolio. As part of her report, she needs to calculate the profit and loss.

**Scenario:**

She had a stock that was valued at $12.75 per share and dropped by $4.80. 

Then, she invested in a new company whose stock price increased from $7.95 by $3.20.

What is the net impact on the value of her investments?

**A) $0.00**  
**B) $2.15**  
**C) $1.15**  
**D) $1.60**

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** First figure out how much the first stock changed and the second. Then add them.
- **Level 2:** The first stock went from $12.75 to $12.75 - $4.80 = $7.95. The second stock went from $7.95 to $7.95 + $3.20 = $11.15. Now, what’s the difference?
- **Level 3:** Calculate the net loss/gain for each transaction first, then add them together. (Loss is negative, gain is positive).

## Stage 4: Practice

**Problem 1:**

James is skiing on a mountain in Quebec. The elevation changes drastically in the course of his trip.

He begins at -18.3 meters (below sea level), descends to -12.6 meters, and then climbs to 5.7 meters.

What was the total change in elevation?

**A) 36.6 meters**  
**B) 3.1 meters**  
**C) 13.3 meters**  
**D) 24.6 meters**

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** Track each elevation change separately: from -18.3 to -12.6, and then from -12.6 to 5.7.
- **Level 2:** You can think of it as two different "steps." Calculate how far he went in each step and add.
- **Level 3:** The total change is how far he went from the start to the end — from -18.3 to 5.7. What is the distance between them on the number line?

**Problem 2:**

A math teacher in Ontario is calculating the average of her students’ quiz grades.

She had 3 scores: -2.5 points (below average), +1.8 points, and -0.3 points.

What is the net change in points for this group of quizzes?

**A) -1.0 points**  
**B) -0.3 points**  
**C) -1.3 points**  
**D) -1.8 points**

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** Add all the changes together.
- **Level 2:** Think of it in terms of debt vs. credit. Subtract the total losses from the total gains.
- **Level 3:** Let’s list all numbers: -2.5 + 1.8 + (-0.3). Add them step by step, watching the signs.

**Problem 3:**

A student in Winnipeg is planning a road trip. The odometer reads 142.8 km initially.

They drive for 1.2 hours at a negative rate of -85.6 km/h (a turn back from their original direction).

What is the final distance of their odometer reading?

**A) -75.2 km**  
**B) 219.4 km**  
**C) 57.2 km**  
**D) 57.6 km**

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** If they're going at -85.6 km/h, they're going back in the opposite direction.
- **Level 2:** Multiply the speed × time, but since it’s negative, it means they went back.
- **Level 3:** Initial reading: 142.8 km. Then they go back: 142.8 - (85.6 × 1.2).

## Stage 5: Capstone

**Capstone Problem:**

A student in Saskatoon is tracking the changes in their monthly budget.

In January, she spent $120.30 on groceries and earned $80.00 from tutoring.

In February, she spent $95.45 and earned $150.00.

In March, she spent $110.50 and earned $65.00.

What is her **net savings** over these three months?

**A) $130.75**  
**B) $100.00**  
**C) $100.25**  
**D) $85.50**

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** First, calculate for each month how much money she had left or spent.
- **Level 2:** Think of it in terms of positive and negative numbers — she's earning (positive) and spending (negative).
- **Level 3:** For each month, add earnings and subtract expenses. Total net = sum of the monthly net amounts.

**Single guiding question (between attempt 1 and 2 only):**
> “What are the net earnings for each month? What is the sum of your three net amounts?”

**If passed:**
> "You've solved a complex, multi-step real-world problem that combines decimals, addition, subtraction, and financial understanding—all without needing extra help. That’s strong math and you did it well."

**If failed after 2 attempts:**
> "This problem had many steps and was genuinely challenging. We'll come back to it next time—we’ve learned a lot already. Let’s try again when you're ready!"
