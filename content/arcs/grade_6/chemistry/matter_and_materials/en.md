## Stage 1: Hook

**Hook Story 1:**
Meet Alex, an 11-year-old from Vancouver who loves to cook with his mom. One cold winter morning, Alex watched his mom take a pot of water from the fridge and put it on the stove. The ice cube in the pot began to melt, turning from a solid into a liquid. Alex was curious — why did this happen? What changed inside the ice cube?

**Hook Story 2:**
Imagine you're at a summer camp in Ontario and you’re helping to set up campfires. You notice that when you light a match, it creates a flame — a gas — that rises up into the air. However, if you pour cold water onto the fire, the gas turns back into liquid droplets. It's fascinating how matter can change its form!

**Objective:** Introduce the concept that matter can exist in different forms and that temperature affects how matter changes.

## Stage 2: Concept

**Topic: States of Matter**

**Lesson Content:**

**Solid**: Has a definite shape and volume. The particles are close together and vibrate in place.
  
**Liquid**: Has a definite volume but no definite shape. The particles are close together but can move around each other.
  
**Gas**: Has no definite shape or volume. The particles are far apart and move freely around.

**Temperature Effects:** As temperature increases (heating), matter generally changes from solid to liquid to gas. As temperature decreases (cooling), it changes from gas to liquid to solid.

**Particle Model of Matter:**
- Particles are tiny things that make up everything we see.
- In solids, particles are tightly packed and vibrate in one spot.
- In liquids, particles are close together and can slide past each other.
- In gases, particles are far apart and move quickly in all directions.

**Intervention Hints:**
- Level 1: “Think of a snowball for a solid, milk for a liquid, and air for a gas. What can't you hold in your hands?”
- Level 2: “Where are the particles closest together? In a solid or a gas?”
- Level 3: “What makes particles move faster or slower? How does temperature affect their motion?”

**Comprehension Check:**

**Question:** What is the main difference between a solid and a gas?

A) Solids have a definite shape and volume.  
B) Gases have a definite shape and volume.  
C) Solids are hard and gases are soft.  
D) Gases can flow but solids cannot.

**Correct Answer:** A

**Common Misconception Distractors:**
- B (Incorrectly stating gas properties)
- C (Mixing matter properties with texture)
- D (Confusing flow with state characteristics)

## Stage 3: Guided Problem

**Question: You are observing an ice cube melting in your hand. What happens to the ice cube's particles as it melts?**

A) They move closer together.  
B) They move faster and spread out.  
C) They move slower and vibrate in place.  
D) They stay the same and don’t move.

**Correct Answer:** B

**Intervention Hints:**
- Level 1: “If you heat the ice cube, do the particles move slower or faster?”
- Level 2: “Why does the solid change into a liquid? What do you think happens to particles?”
- Level 3: “In the solid state, particles have less energy. In the liquid state, what do you think happens to particles’ energy?”

**Common Misconception Distractors:**
- A (Misunderstanding that particles get closer during melting)
- C (Thinking particles slow down in liquid)
- D (Not understanding that particles move during phase changes)

## Stage 4: Practice

**Problem 1: What happens to matter when heat is added?**

A) It becomes a gas.
B) It always becomes a liquid.
C) It usually becomes more energetic and can change states.
D) It becomes less flexible and harder.

**Correct Answer:** C

**Intervention Hints:**
- Level 1: “Try thinking of what happens to ice water when you leave it out in the sun.”
- Level 2: “How does heat make the particles in ice move?”
- Level 3: “When you add energy, what usually happens to the state of matter?”

**Common Misconception Distractors:**
- A (Oversimplifying state change)
- B (Believing matter only forms a liquid)
- D (Misunderstanding heat effects on material properties)

**Problem 2: Which property of a material describes how easily it can be bent?**

A) Hardness  
B) Conductivity  
C) Flexibility  
D) Density

**Correct Answer:** C

**Intervention Hints:**
- Level 1: “Think about a rubber band — is it hard or flexible?”
- Level 2: “If a material can bend easily, what is that called?”
- Level 3: “What is the main characteristic of flexible materials?”

**Common Misconception Distractors:**
- A (Confusing hardness with flexibility)
- B (Mixing up conductivity and bending)
- D (Thinking flexibility relates to density)

**Problem 3: What is the best example of matter changing from a gas to a solid?**

A) Baking a cake  
B) Freezing water  
C) Steam condensing into water droplets  
D) Melting ice

**Correct Answer:** C

**Intervention Hints:**
- Level 1: “What happens to steam (gas) when it hits a cold window?”
- Level 2: “What is this process called when a gas turns into a liquid?”
- Level 3: “Which change involves a gas cooling down and forming a solid? Think about what happens around ice cubes or hot drinks.”

**Common Misconception Distractors:**
- A (Incorrectly identifying cooking as gas to solid conversion)
- B (Mixing freezing with gas to solid)
- D (Confusing melting with gas to solid transition)

## Stage 5: Capstone

**Problem:**
Sarah is making homemade ice cream. She puts the ice cream mix in the freezer. The mixture changes from a liquid to a solid form.

1. What property of matter changes when the liquid ice cream mix turns to solid ice cream?
   A) Color  
   B) Temperature  
   C) State  
   D) Volume

**Correct Answer:** C

2. What causes the ice cream mix to become solid?
   A) Cooling (removing heat)  
   B) Heating (adding heat)  
   C) Stirring  
   D) Evaporation

**Correct Answer:** A

**Guiding Question (If student struggles between attempt 1 and 2):**
"Do you know that cooling removes heat from matter? This is how a liquid becomes a solid."

**If Passed:**
"Well done! You just applied your understanding of states of matter and temperature effects. You're showing a deep understanding of how the world works."

**If Failed After 2 Attempts:**
"That’s a tough capstone! You've learned really well, and we’ll come back to this challenge next time. Let’s practice a bit more with the science of changing matter before we finish."

**Alternate Capstone (if reattempting):**
**Problem:**
You are cooking eggs for breakfast. You notice that when you crack an egg into a pan and heat it, the white becomes white and solid, while the yolk remains yellow and somewhat runny. What explains this difference?

A) One is solid and one is liquid  
B) The proteins in each part respond differently to heat  
C) The yolk has more heat than the white  
D) The yolk is harder to cook

**Correct Answer:** B

**Guiding Question for Alternate:**
"Think about the materials of the egg — what parts of the egg are different in how they respond to heat?"
