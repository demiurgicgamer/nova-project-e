## S1 Hook

**Hook Title:** What's in Your Lunch Box?

**Hook Description:**
Let's look at the ingredients in your lunch today. You might have a sandwich with ham and cheese, a piece of fruit, and a juice box. But wait — what ingredients actually make up those items? Are they all made of just one type of matter, or do they mix together? We're going to explore how different things come together to make the world around us.

Here's a real-life example — imagine you've just finished a long summer day at the beach. You're hungry and want some water, but there's a problem — the water isn't clean. You found a bottle and it looks like it's full of something, but what is it really? We're going to discover all about how things mix and how to separate them using the science of mixtures and solutions.

**Hook Story 1:**
You're back from a family camping trip with your sibling. You're excited to see the lake because you love fishing at the same spot you’ve been going to for years. But when you get there, you notice the water looks strange. You see oil on the surface. Your sibling says, "That’s okay, we can fish anyway." But you've heard from your teacher that sometimes water has things in it that are harmful — things like bacteria or tiny plastic pieces. What do scientists do to figure out what’s really in the water before anyone drinks it?

**Hook Story 2:**
Imagine you're at a local market in a Canadian town. You see a vendor selling maple syrup. He tells you it takes 40 gallons of sap from a sugar maple tree to make just 1 gallon of syrup. He’s explaining how the sap turns into syrup — a solution — through a process of heating and evaporation. What happens to the water when the sap is heated? Also, what's really *in* your maple syrup? Is it just sugar water? Or are there other ingredients or impurities? 

**Outcome:**
These real-life situations introduce the concept of mixtures and solutions. Children are naturally curious when they see things they recognize, like their own lunches or the things they encounter at camp or the market.

## S2 Concept

**Concept Title:** Understanding Mixtures and Solutions

**Concept Description:**
A mixture is two or more different substances that are combined but not chemically bonded. You can see what’s inside, and the parts can be separated by physical means. For example, a trail mix or a sandwich. Solutions are a special type of mixture where one substance dissolves in another. In a solution, you can't see the separate parts. Salt-water or kool-aid are examples.

There are two main kinds of mixtures:
- Homogeneous: You can't see the parts — like salt in water, or air.
- Heterogeneous: You can see the parts — like sand and water, or trail mix.

A solution always has:
- Solvent (the substance that does the dissolving, usually water)
- Solute (the substance that is dissolved into the solvent)

A solution also has solubility — how much of the solute can dissolve in the solvent.

Let's practice sorting different things to understand how they act as mixtures or solutions.

**Comprehension Check:**

**Question 1:**
You have a glass with water and sand in it. This is an example of what type of mixture?
A) Homogeneous  
B) Solution  
C) Heterogeneous  
D) Pure Substance  

**Correct Answer:** C) Heterogeneous

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** Are you looking at the parts of the mixture? Can you see the sand?  
- **Level 2:** Can you see the sand in the water? Is it evenly mixed like a solution?  
- **Level 3:** When the sand is on the bottom and the water is on top, is it the same throughout?  

**Question 2:**
Which of the following is a solution?
A) Oil and water  
B) Soil and water  
C) Salt in water  
D) Rock and sand  

**Correct Answer:** C) Salt in water

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** What happens when you add salt to water? Do you see the salt?  
- **Level 2:** When salt dissolves in water, does it form one thing or stay visible?  
- **Level 3:** Is salt in water a solution — can you see the sand or salt clearly?  

**Question 3:**
What is the solvent in a sugar-water solution?
A) Sugar  
B) Water  
C) Salt  
D) Air  

**Correct Answer:** B) Water

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** What dissolves the sugar?  
- **Level 2:** In sugar-water, which part breaks down the sugar and dissolves it?  
- **Level 3:** Which substance is doing most of the work?  

**Outcome:**
Children learn to identify the differences between mixtures and solutions, and between homogeneous and heterogeneous mixtures. The idea of what a solvent and solute are is introduced. The comprehension check allows for reinforcement of key vocabulary and concepts.

## S3 Guided

**Problem:**
You are given a mixture of soil and water. What would you expect to happen after you let it settle for a few minutes?
A) The soil will dissolve into the water  
B) The soil will settle to the bottom and the water will remain clear  
C) The water will evaporate  
D) The soil and water will form a new substance

**Correct Answer:** B) The soil will settle to the bottom and the water will remain clear  

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** What happens to soil when it is put in water? Can you see it clearly?  
- **Level 2:** Is there a way that soil and water can mix evenly?  
- **Level 3:** When you look at a mixture of soil and water, what do you see at the bottom?  

**Problem:**
When you pour lemon juice into a glass of water, it dissolves and you can no longer see the lemon juice. Which of these best describes what happens?
A) Lemon juice and water form a heterogeneous mixture  
B) Lemon juice and water form a solution  
C) Lemon juice and water form a pure substance  
D) Lemon juice and water form a heterogeneous mixture

**Correct Answer:** B) Lemon juice and water form a solution

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** When lemon juice dissolves in water, is it visible anymore?  
- **Level 2:** Did the lemon juice form a new thing or did it mix with water?  
- **Level 3:** Are you now looking at one thing or two separate things that you can still see clearly?  

**Problem:**
Which of these is a homogeneous mixture?
A) Sand in water  
B) Oil and water  
C) Air  
D) Soil and water  

**Correct Answer:** C) Air  

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** Can you see the parts of air?  
- **Level 2:** Are the parts in air the same throughout like a solution?  
- **Level 3:** When you look at air, do you see the nitrogen, the oxygen, and the other gases?  

**Outcome:**
Students build on their understanding of solutions and mixtures by seeing how they behave in real situations, making real decisions about what types of mixtures they are working with. Each problem allows guided support and practice with critical thinking.

## S4 Practice

**Problem 1:**
You pour sugar into water and stir it until it dissolves completely. What kind of mixture have you created?
A) Homogeneous  
B) Heterogeneous  
C) Solution  
D) Pure substance  

**Correct Answer:** C) Solution  

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** Did the sugar dissolve completely and can you not see it?  
- **Level 2:** What happens when sugar dissolves in water? Is it like soil in water?  
- **Level 3:** What word best describes when one thing dissolves into another and we can't see it?  

**Problem 2:**
You are given a solution of salt in water. Which method would be best to separate the salt from the water?
A) Filtering  
B) Evaporation  
C) Distillation  
D) Magnetic separation  

**Correct Answer:** B) Evaporation  

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** What happens to the water when heat is applied to it?  
- **Level 2:** Can you take out the salt after it's dissolved or does it stay mixed?  
- **Level 3:** What method allows you to collect the water back while leaving the salt behind?  

**Problem 3:**
Which of the following is a heterogeneous mixture?
A) Salt water  
B) Air  
C) Milk  
D) Trail mix  

**Correct Answer:** D) Trail mix  

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** Do you easily see the different parts of trail mix?  
- **Level 2:** When you mix trail mix, do the parts blend together like water and sugar?  
- **Level 3:** Is trail mix the same throughout like a solution or can you see the parts?  

**Outcome:**
These practice questions build confidence and reinforce the understanding of how mixtures and solutions are identified, what they look like, and how to separate them. Each question is designed to challenge reasoning and application, with correct answers supported by appropriate intervention support.

## S5 Application

**Problem:**
You are at a nature camp. Your instructor brings you to a stream. You notice the water looks cloudy. She says it’s likely full of particles that are too small for your eyes to see. She tells you that they can be removed by letting the water settle. What is the best way to clean the water and separate the small particles from it?
A) Salt and water  
B) Filter the water  
C) Evaporate the water  
D) Boil the water  

**Correct Answer:** B) Filter the water  

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** What does a filter do with small particles?  
- **Level 2:** Can you get rid of the fine particles by letting it settle?  
- **Level 3:** How do you separate something that cannot be seen?  

**Problem:**
You decide to make your own kool-aid. You add kool-aid powder to water and stir. What happens to the kool-aid powder?
A) It remains solid and you can still see it  
B) It dissolves and becomes part of the water  
C) It mixes but stays separated  
D) It changes into a new chemical substance  

**Correct Answer:** B) It dissolves and becomes part of the water  

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** What does it mean when something dissolves?  
- **Level 2:** Do you see the kool-aid powder after stirring?  
- **Level 3:** Did it form a solution where the powder and water became one?  

**Problem:**
You are preparing a lunch. You are making trail mix that has nuts, seeds, and dried fruit. Is this a solution or a heterogeneous mixture?
A) Solution  
B) Heterogeneous mixture  
C) Homogeneous mixture  
D) Pure substance  

**Correct Answer:** B) Heterogeneous mixture  

**Interventions:**
- **Level 1:** Do you see the nuts, seeds and fruit in the trail mix?  
- **Level 2:** Do they blend together or remain separate?  
- **Level 3:** Is the trail mix evenly mixed, like a solution, or visible, like a heterogeneous mixture?  

**Outcome:**
These application problems challenge students to use their knowledge in practical, real-life scenarios. Students apply their understanding of mixtures, solutions, and their separation through a problem-solving lens, showing mastery and practical understanding of science concepts.

## S6 Wrap-up

**Review Activity:**

**Title:** Sorting the Mixtures

**Description:**
Students receive a list of items: salt in water, soil in water, kool-aid in water, trail mix, and air. They will group them into the following categories: solution, heterogeneous mixture, and homogeneous mixture.

Students will also identify the solvent and solute for each situation.

**Guided Questions:**
1. Which items are solutions?
2. Which items are heterogeneous mixtures?
3. Which items are homogeneous mixtures?
4. What is the solvent in each solution?
5. What is the solute in each solution?

**Conclusion:**
Understanding how mixtures and solutions work is important for science. It helps explain what’s in our food, our water, and even the air we breathe. We use this information in our daily lives — from making kool-aid to cleaning our homes. In science, we use separation methods like stirring, filtering, and evaporation to find out what's in things, so we can keep our environment safe and clean.

--- 

**Outcome:**
Students engage in meaningful discussion and reinforcement of what they've learned about mixtures, solutions, and separation techniques. The wrap-up provides a summary, a reflection of content, and a final assessment of their learning. This helps build a foundation for more advanced chemistry concepts.
