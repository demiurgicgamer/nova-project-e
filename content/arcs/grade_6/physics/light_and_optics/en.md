## Stage 2: Concept

### Concept: Light Travels in Straight Lines

Today we're going to look at how light moves. You already know that we see things when light enters our eyes, but have you ever wondered if light has a direction?

Light always travels **in straight lines** through air and transparent materials like glass.

#### Example
- When you shine a flashlight into a room, the beam travels straight.
- If you look at the beam, you’ll see it in a straight line, not curved.

Try this at home:
- Shine a flashlight through a clear glass of water. The light beam travels in one straight line through the glass.

We call this **the path of light**, and it always bends only when it moves from one material to another (like air to water).

#### Vocabulary:
- **Light ray** – a line showing the direction light travels.
- **Transparent** – a material that allows light to pass through (like water, glass).
- **Straight line** – a path that doesn’t curve or bend.

### Comprehension Check

#### Question:
What path does light take when moving through air?

A) Curves around objects  
B) Bends at every corner  
C) Travels in straight lines  
D) Jumps from one side to the other  

### Intervention Hints

- **Level 1:** Think back to when you shine a flashlight – does the beam stay straight or does it bend?
- **Level 2:** If you look at a light source (like a lamp), can you see light in a straight line? Try turning off the lights and shining the light.
- **Level 3:** Try drawing the path of light. Start with a light source and draw lines outward. Does the light bend?

## Stage 3: Guided

### Guided Problem

#### Question:
You are walking near a lake and notice that the bottom of the water isn’t visible directly. Why is this?

A) Light bends when it hits the water  
B) Light travels in straight lines so you can only see what's directly in front  
C) It’s too dark in the water  
D) The bottom of the lake is too far away  

### Intervention Hints

- **Level 1:** Did you see the stick appear bent at the water surface? Why did that happen?
- **Level 2:** Light bends when it moves from air to water. What happens to the light that tries to reach the bottom of the lake?
- **Level 3:** When light hits water, it **bends downward**, which means we can’t see what is directly beneath us.

## Stage 4: Practice

### Practice Problem 1

#### Question:
A beam of light is shone on a mirror. The light bounces off the surface and continues in a new direction. What is this called?

A) Reflection  
B) Refraction  
C) Absorption  
D) Dispersion  

### Intervention Hints

- **Level 1:** Did you ever see yourself in a mirror? What did the light do?
- **Level 2:** When light hits a smooth surface and bounces back, we call that reflection.
- **Level 3:** If light hits a mirror and changes direction, but goes back toward the source, that's what we call **reflection**.

### Practice Problem 2

#### Question:
Which of the following materials would allow you to see light traveling through it?

A) A stone  
B) A wooden block  
C) Clear glass  
D) A fabric  

### Intervention Hints

- **Level 1:** Can light travel through clear glass or a block of wood?
- **Level 2:** Materials that let light pass through are called transparent.
- **Level 3:** If something looks clear and you can see through it, light is allowed to pass through it.

### Practice Problem 3

#### Question:
You see a rainbow after a rainstorm. What causes the colors of the rainbow?

A) Reflection of light in water droplets  
B) The color of the sky changing  
C) Sunlight bending and separating through water droplets  
D) Light from clouds mixing with sunlight  

### Intervention Hints

- **Level 1:** What happens to light when it hits a droplet of water?
- **Level 2:** Did you ever see light bending through a prism? Same idea!
- **Level 3:** When sunlight goes through millions of tiny water droplets in the air, it **bends and separates into different colors**.

## Stage 5: Capstone

### Capstone Problem

#### Question:
You're building a telescope to look at stars, and want to use two lenses. The lens that collects the light is convex, and the lens that magnifies the image is also convex. What is the main function of a convex lens?

A) It bends light outwards  
B) It makes objects appear larger  
C) It bends light inwards  
D) It makes everything look smaller  

### Intervention Hints

- **Level 1:** Did you ever use a magnifying glass? What happens when you look at something through it?
- **Level 2:** Convex lenses are curved inward – they gather or focus light rays to a point.
- **Level 3:** Think about how a magnifying glass focuses light to make images appear larger.

### Single Guiding Question (between attempt 1 and 2 attempts only)

> "What is the shape of a convex lens, and what does it do to light rays?"
