## Stage 1: Hook

**Hook Story:**

In an icy winter morning, a young skater named Amara is practicing on the local hockey rink. She pushes off from the boards with her stick. She glides smoothly across the ice without using her hands. But after a few seconds, she begins to slow down and eventually stops. Why does this happen?

What makes Amara start moving? What makes her stop? What is the invisible thing that helps her move and what might bring her to a stop?

**Hook Story 2:**

In a backyard in Thunder Bay, a child named Kael is trying to push a heavy sled loaded with toys up a snowy hill. Even though he pushes as hard as he can, the sled doesn’t move very far. Why is that?

What force is being applied? Is there something else that’s making it hard for the sled to move forward? What kind of force could help him push the sled up the hill?

## Stage 2: Concept

**Objective:** Understand what a force is, the difference between contact and non-contact forces, common forces like gravity, friction, and air resistance, and how forces affect motion.

**Concept Check:**
What is a force? 

A. A pull or a push on an object  
B. Something that makes an object change color  
C. A sound that a moving object makes  
D. A light that makes things bright  

✅ Correct Answer: A

**Interventions:**

- **Level 1:** Think of when you push a door open. What are you doing to the door?  
- **Level 2:** Can you think of something you pull? How about a toy train on a track, what pulls it along?  
- **Level 3:** Forces can be described by how they make things move. If you push a toy car, what happens?

## Stage 3: Guided Problem

**Problem:**

Sofia is sliding down a playground slide. As she goes down, she starts to slow down. Which force is mainly causing her to slow down?

A. Gravity  
B. Friction  
C. Air resistance  
D. Her push on the slide  

✅ Correct Answer: B

**Interventions:**

- **Level 1:** Friction is often a problem on slides. What happens when you put sandpaper on a slide?  
- **Level 2:** The slide doesn’t stay smooth when Sofia is sliding on it. That’s friction.  
- **Level 3:** Think about how you go down a slide. What stops you at the end?

## Stage 4: Practice

**Problem 1:**

Which of the following is an example of a non-contact force?  

A. A baseball being hit by a bat  
B. A child pushing a shopping cart  
C. A magnet attracting a nail  
D. A hand pulling a sled  

✅ Correct Answer: C

**Interventions:**

- **Level 1:** Think about forces that work without touching. When you use a magnet, does it have to touch the nail?  
- **Level 2:** Which of these forces is acting without you touching the object?  
- **Level 3:** A magnet can pull on something without actually touching it. That is a non-contact force.  

---

**Problem 2:**

A hockey puck on the ice is gliding toward the goal. What force will MOST likely cause it to stop?  

A. Gravity  
B. Friction  
C. Air resistance  
D. A player’s stick  

✅ Correct Answer: B

**Interventions:**

- **Level 1:** What happens when something rolls over sand? It slows down.  
- **Level 2:** Ice is smooth — it’s not like sand, so which force helps things stop?  
- **Level 3:** The puck is sliding — it needs what kind of force to slow it down?  

---

**Problem 3:**

Which of the following forces is acting when a ball falls from your hand?  

A. Air resistance  
B. Friction  
C. Gravity  
D. A pull from a magnet  

✅ Correct Answer: C

**Interventions:**

- **Level 1:** When you drop something, what makes it fall?  
- **Level 2:** Gravity is the unseen force that pulls things to the ground.  
- **Level 3:** When you throw something up, what pulls it back down?

## Stage 5: Capstone

**Problem:**

In a physics experiment, two cars are moving toward each other on a track. One car is moving fast and the other is moving slowly. When they crash, what will cause the cars to change direction?

A. Gravity alone  
B. Balanced forces between the cars  
C. Air resistance  
D. Unbalanced forces between the cars  

✅ Correct Answer: D

**Interventions:**

- **Level 1:** Did you ever see a ball bounce off a wall? Why did it go in a new direction?  
- **Level 2:** If two forces are acting on something but they're the same, what does that cause?  
- **Level 3:** In a car crash, what does it mean when forces are different?  

**Guiding Question (between Attempt 1 and 2):**

> What forces are acting on each car? Are these forces equal or different?

**If passed:**

> Well done — you’ve just solved a real physics situation! You needed to understand how forces change motion and how unbalanced forces make things change direction. This kind of thinking is how Scientists make sense of our world.

**If failed after 2 attempts:**

> This problem was a bit more complex, involving both direction and force balance. You've already learned a lot today. Let’s come back to this next session, and we’ll work through it together.

**Alternate Capstone:**

A child pushes a toy car forward, and the car moves in the same direction. What could explain why the car stops after moving forward?

A. A push in the opposite direction  
B. Friction  
C. Gravity  
D. An unbalanced force  

✅ Correct Answer: B
