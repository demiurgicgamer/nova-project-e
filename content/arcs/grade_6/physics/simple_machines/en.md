## Stage 1: Hook

**Hook Story 1 - Canada's Building Blocks**

It's a quiet morning in Toronto. Maya is at the construction site helping her dad with a new park. There are big wooden beams that need to be lifted onto a platform. The workers are using a lever, a long piece of wood, to help lift the heavy beam. Maya watches, curious. Her dad says, "When you push down on one end, the other end goes up. It helps us move heavy things without using too much effort!" 

The next day, Maya goes to a local museum. She sees a model of an ancient Egyptian pyramid being built. The workers used ramps — a type of simple machine called an inclined plane — to carry stones up to the top of the pyramid, instead of lifting them straight up. Maya wonders, "What would happen if they used a pulley instead?"

**Hook Story 2 - Arctic Exploration**

In the Yukon, Nyla is learning about the tools her grandmother used for building a shelter. Her grandmother explains that she used a wedge — like a sharp rock or blade — to split logs into smaller pieces. When the wedge is pushed into wood, it pushes the wood apart. Then, using a screw — the same kind you use on a bottle cap — she could tighten different parts and make her shelter secure.

Nyla sees something strange at a local outdoor exhibition. A display has several wheels and axles, like the ones used in sleds. Her guide says, "You might not think a wheel and axle helps you move things, but it actually reduces effort in a big way!" 

These examples show how simple machines have been used by people in different parts of Canada and around the world for thousands of years!

**Observation/Question Prompt:**
> Can you think of other situations where you've seen simple machines in action?
> Do you think they help us do things easier, faster, or both?

## Stage 2: Concept

**Concept Introduction:**

Simple machines are basic tools that help us do work more easily. There are six main types: the lever, wheel and axle, pulley, inclined plane, wedge, and screw. Each one works in a different way to reduce the effort we need to do.

Let’s explore how they work:

1. **Lever**: A rigid rod that pivots around a point (fulcrum). It helps lift heavy objects using less force.
2. **Wheel and Axle**: A wheel attached to a shaft (axle). It makes it easier to move large weights by reducing friction.
3. **Pulley**: A wheel with a rope or cable around it. It changes the direction of force.
4. **Inclined Plane**: A slanted surface that reduces effort needed to move things upward.
5. **Wedge**: A thick object that ends in a thin edge. It pushes material apart.
6. **Screw**: A slanted surface wrapped around a cylinder. It can hold things securely and lift things.

Each machine increases your mechanical advantage, which means it allows you to do the same work using less applied effort.

**Comprehension Check:**
Which part of a lever can move up and down to help lift heavy things?

A) Fulcrum  
B) Effort arm  
C) Load arm  
D) Pivot point

**Intervention Hints:**
Level 1: "Think about what part of a lever moves when you lift something."  
Level 2: "Try putting a pencil under a ruler and lifting from the top — which part goes up?"  
Level 3: "Imagine if you had a stick and placed a rock underneath — can you tell which end moves most?"

## Stage 3: Guided

**Guided Problem:**

A carpenter is building a fence and needs to lift a heavy post. They choose to use a lever to make this easier.

How does the lever help the carpenter?

A) By increasing the distance the effort arm moves  
B) By reducing the amount of force needed  
C) By making the work harder  
D) By using more energy  

**Intervention Hints:**
Level 1: "What is the main job of a lever in a real-life situation?"  
Level 2: "If you wanted to open a tight lid or lift a heavy rock, would you choose a lever or push with your hands?"  
Level 3: "Try using a ruler as if it were a lever — can you see how one side goes up while the other goes down?"

## Stage 4: Practice

### Problem 4a — Real-World Simple Machines

Which simple machine is the best example of an inclined plane?

A) A bottle cap  
B) A hammer  
C) A ramp for wheelchair access  
D) A door knob  

**Intervention Hints:**
Level 1: "Can you think of a slanted surface that helps you move something up?"  
Level 2: "Look through your home or school — what slanted thing can raise things up?"  
Level 3: "If you had to move heavy boxes from the ground to a shelf without using a ramp, what would you have to do?"

**Practice Problem 4b — Mechanical Advantage**

Which of the following simple machines gives the greatest mechanical advantage?

A) Lever  
B) Inclined plane  
C) Pulley  
D) Screw  

**Intervention Hints:**
Level 1: "Which machine helps you use less effort?"  
Level 2: "Which one is the easiest to move something heavy when it's far from the fulcrum?"  
Level 3: "Try using a long lever compared to a short lever and see if the effort is different."

**Practice Problem 4c — Everyday Tools**

A kitchen knife is a type of which simple machine?

A) Wedge  
B) Pulley  
C) Screw  
D) Inclined plane  

**Intervention Hints:**
Level 1: "Does it cut into things — like how an axe splits wood?"  
Level 2: "What shape does the edge of a knife have?"  
Level 3: "Would you use a knife to lift something, or to cut it open?"

## Stage 5: Capstone

### Capstone Problem

The school science fair is coming up, and you're building a model of a crane for a presentation. You decide to use a group of simple machines.

Which combination of simple machines would make a crane that lifts a heavy object using the least effort possible?

A) Lever + Pulley  
B) Inclined plane + Screw  
C) Wedge + Pulley  
D) Wheel and axle + Lever  

**Intervention Hints (if needed):**
Level 1: "Think about a crane. What does it do? Does it pull things up or move things along the floor?"  
Level 2: "What machines help lift a load up and down?"  
Level 3: "Which machines reduce effort by changing the direction or increasing force?"
