## Stage 1: Hook

**Hook Story Title:** "Electrical Wonders: Powering Your Home"

*Imagine this — you're at home and you flip a switch. The lights go on. You take out your phone to watch a video, and it powers up. You turn on the microwave for a snack. All of these things use something called *electricity*. But what exactly is electricity? How does it move through wires to power the things you use every day?*

*In many homes in Canada, especially in the provinces like Ontario, Quebec, and British Columbia, you get electricity from power lines that run through your streets. These lines bring electricity into your home from power plants far away. And in some areas, people live in cabins or small homes with solar panels, or even use generators during power outages — this gives them an extra understanding of how electricity works at home.*

*So in this arc, you will learn about the basics of electricity — how it flows, how it powers things, and how we can stay safe when working with it.*

**Key Concepts Introduced:**
- Electricity powers our homes and devices.
- Electrical energy is invisible but useful.
- We use a mix of power sources — from power plants to solar panels.

## Stage 2: Concept

**Concept Title:** Understanding the Flow of Electricity

**Learning Goal:**
Children will understand that electricity flows through circuits, and will be introduced to the basic terms *current*, *voltage*, and *resistance*, in simple terms.

**Teaching Content:**
Electricity is the flow of tiny particles called electrons. When electrons move through a conductor (like a wire), we call that electric current. Voltage is like the "push" that makes the electrons move — it’s the force behind the electricity. Resistance is the opposition to the flow of electricity — some materials make it harder for electrons to pass through, like rubber or wood.

We can think of electricity like water flowing in a pipe:
- The **current** is how much water flows.
- The **voltage** is how hard the water is being pushed.
- The **resistance** is how much the pipe narrows or gets blocked.

**Comprehension Check Question:**

Which of the following BEST describes voltage in a circuit?

A) The speed at which electrons move  
B) The force that pushes electrons to move  
C) The amount of electricity stored in a battery  
D) The resistance that stops current flow  

**Intervention Hints:**
- **Level 1:** Think about water in a pipe: what helps the water move?  
- **Level 2:** Voltage is like a water pump — it makes the water move through the pipe.  
- **Level 3:** If you have a strong pump (high voltage) vs. a weak pump (low voltage), what does that do to the water flow?

## Stage 3: Guided

**Guided Problem:**

**Scenario:** You're helping your dad wire up a new light fixture. You find a wire that has a label saying "copper." Your dad tells you that copper is a good conductor. What does that mean?

A) It blocks electricity  
B) It allows electricity to flow easily  
C) It stores electricity  
D) It makes it harder for electrons to pass through  

**Intervention Hints:**
- **Level 1:** What happens when you try to push a toy through a smooth surface vs. a rough one?  
- **Level 2:** Think about the materials in your classroom — which ones let electricity flow easily vs. ones that stop it.  
- **Level 3:** Copper is used in wires because it easily lets electrons move through it — it's not hard to push electrons through it.

## Stage 4: Practice

### Problem A: Simple Circuit

**Scenario:** You are building a simple circuit with a battery, a bulb, and a wire. When you complete the circuit, the bulb lights up. What must happen in order for the bulb to light up?

A) Only the battery needs to be present  
B) The circuit must be complete (no gaps)  
C) The wire must be made of rubber  
D) The bulb must be a fluorescent type  

**Intervention Hints:**
- **Level 1:** Imagine if someone broke a string in a flashlight — what happens?  
- **Level 2:** A closed loop is needed for electricity to flow. It’s like a full circle or path.  
- **Level 3:** Electricity needs a full path, like a river needing a full channel so water can flow from one end to the other.

---

### Problem B: Series vs Parallel

**Scenario:** You are building a string of holiday lights. You notice that if one bulb burns out, all the others go out, too. What type of circuit do these lights use?

A) A parallel circuit  
B) A series circuit  
C) An open circuit  
D) A resistor circuit  

**Intervention Hints:**
- **Level 1:** What happens in a chain of friendship bracelets when one breaks?  
- **Level 2:** In a series circuit, electricity must go through all parts one by one. If one breaks, the whole path stops.  
- **Level 3:** Think of a water pipe system — if you block one part, the water stops everywhere after that.

---

### Problem C: Conductors vs Insulators

**Scenario:** You're testing a new tool with a circuit tester. You touch one part with a metal spoon and another with a wooden stick. What will happen?

A) Both will light up the tester  
B) Neither will light up  
C) The metal spoon will light up, the wooden stick won't  
D) The wooden stick will light up, the metal spoon won't  

**Intervention Hints:**
- **Level 1:** Which of these would let water through: a solid rock or a garden hose?  
- **Level 2:** Metals allow electricity to flow easily, wood blocks electricity.  
- **Level 3:** Look in your home — what objects are made of metal that let electricity through, and what objects are made of wood, plastic, or rubber that block it?

## Stage 5: Capstone

### Capstone Problem:

**Scenario:** You're planning a small science fair project — a flashlight powered by a battery. You connect the battery to a bulb using wires. You test one wire and the bulb lights up. Then, you add another wire in the same path, but the bulb does not light up anymore. What is likely the issue?

A) The battery is too old  
B) The wires are made of plastic  
C) You've broken the circuit  
D) You switched to a different type of bulb  

**Single guiding question (between attempt 1 and attempt 2 only):**
> What happens to electricity when the circuit is broken?

**If passed:**
> You’ve solved a real life science challenge — understanding what happens in a circuit when a path is disrupted. That shows you’re getting the hang of electricity basics!

**If failed after 2 attempts:**
> This problem asked you to think about how a circuit works when something blocks the flow. Don’t worry — let’s come back to this next time. You’re doing great and have already learned so much.

**Alternate Capstone (for retry):**

**Scenario:** You are helping your friend make a toy car that runs on batteries and has two motors. To make it work, one wire must be connected to each motor. What kind of circuit setup are you using?

A) Series circuit  
B) Parallel circuit  
C) Open circuit  
D) Combination circuit
