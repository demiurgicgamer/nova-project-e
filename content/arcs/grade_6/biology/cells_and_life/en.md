## Stage 1: Hook

### Hook Story

Imagine you're on a field trip to a local science center in Toronto. You enter a large room where there are hundreds of tiny, glowing creatures moving under a microscope. The guide tells you about **cells**, the basic units of life. As you watch, a fish scale falls on the floor near you. The teacher says, “You know what? That scale had cells — and every time you scratch your knee, you’re dealing with the **repairs** of your own cells.”

Suddenly, an alarm rings, and everyone is asked to head to a greenhouse nearby. When you're inside, the guide points to a red maple leaf (a common Canadian tree). “This leaf contains hundreds of **plant cells** – and each cell has special features that help it live.” She tells you that **chloroplasts** inside the leaf help the tree make food using sunlight – just like the solar panels on your home.

You look closely and realize that even the **tiniest organisms**, like bacteria or yeast in the soil, are made of **cells** – and they're alive too.

This is the world of **cells**.

## Stage 2: Concept

### Concept: The Cell as the Basic Unit of Life

**What is a cell?**
A cell is the **basic building block** of all living things. Living things can be made of just one cell, or trillions of cells.

**Types of organisms:**
- **Unicellular organisms** (one cell): Like paramecium or yeast.
- **Multicellular organisms** (many cells): Like humans and trees.

**Plant cells vs Animal cells:**
- **Animal cells** have:
  - Nucleus – contains DNA (the cell's control center)
  - Mitochondria – makes energy (like a power plant)
- **Plant cells** (in addition to animal cell features):
  - Cell wall (protects and gives shape)
  - Chloroplast (makes food using sunlight)

**Functions of Organelles:**
| Organelle | Function |
|-----------|----------|
| Nucleus | Controls cell activities |
| Mitochondria | Makes energy for the cell |
| Cell Wall | Helps support and protect plant cells |
| Chloroplast | Makes food for plant using sunlight |

**Why do cells matter?**
Cells help us grow, heal, and function. When you get a cut, your body uses **cells** to **heal and repair** it. When you eat food, your **cells** break it down and use it for energy and growth.

**Comprehension Check:**
Which of the following best describes what a cell is?

A. A tiny piece of a plant
B. The basic building block of all living things
C. A special kind of food
D. The main part of a machine

**Intervention Hints:**

- **Level 1**: Think about plants, animals, and humans – what are they all made of?
- **Level 2**: You can have cells in your body, or in your food – but what do they all have in common?
- **Level 3**: If you think of a building, the **blocks** that make up the building are like the **cells** in your body.

## Stage 3: Guided

### Guided Problem

**Problem**:  
Lila is looking at a leaf under her microscope. She sees a structure in the cell that allows the plant to make food from sunlight. What is this structure?

A. Mitochondria  
B. Nucleus  
C. Chloroplast  
D. Cell Wall  

**Intervention Hints:**

- **Level 1**: What part of the plant cell makes light into food?
- **Level 2**: The chloroplast is like a kitchen – what does it use?
- **Level 3**: If a leaf were a solar-powered house, what part would catch the sun’s light and turn it into food?

**Answer**: C. Chloroplast  
**Correct Explanation**: Chloroplasts are found in plant cells and use sunlight to make food (glucose) from carbon dioxide and water. This is called photosynthesis.

## Stage 4: Practice

### Practice Problem 1

**Problem**:  
Which of the following is TRUE about both plant and animal cells?

A. They both have chloroplasts  
B. They both have a cell wall  
C. They both have a nucleus  
D. Only animal cells have mitochondria  

**Intervention Hints:**

- **Level 1**: What part is in ALL cells?
- **Level 2**: The nucleus is like the brain – where is it in both types of cells?
- **Level 3**: If a plant and animal are made of cells, which part must they both have?

**Answer**: C. They both have a nucleus  
**Explanation**: The nucleus is found in all living cells (both plant and animal) and holds the DNA that controls cell activities.

---

### Practice Problem 2

**Problem**:  
A student is comparing a cell from a mushroom (a fungus) with a cell from a maple tree. Which structure will be found in BOTH?

A. Cell wall  
B. Chloroplast  
C. Nucleus  
D. Mitochondria  

**Intervention Hints:**

- **Level 1**: Fungi and plants are both living things – what do they always have?
- **Level 2**: What makes the cell work and give energy?
- **Level 3**: If you think of two animals and a tree, which part do they all share?

**Answer**: C. Nucleus  
**Explanation**: Every cell, whether from a tree or a mushroom, has a nucleus to store DNA and control cell activities.

---

### Practice Problem 3

**Problem**:  
A fish scales under a microscope shows hundreds of cells. These cells are part of a:

A. Single cell  
B. Unicellular organism  
C. Multicellular organism  
D. Non-living thing  

**Intervention Hints:**

- **Level 1**: If something is made of many tiny parts, what kind of thing is it?
- **Level 2**: Is a fish one cell or many cells?
- **Level 3**: You’re looking at a scale of a fish – the fish has many cells or just one?

**Answer**: C. Multicellular organism  
**Explanation**: Fish are made of many cells, so they are **multicellular** organisms. Each cell of the scale is one part of a bigger organism.

## Stage 5: Capstone

### Capstone Problem

**Problem**:  
A student is studying two organisms. One is made of just one cell (like an amoeba), and one is made of many cells (like a maple tree). What are they both made of?

A. Both are made of bacteria cells  
B. Both are made of animal cells  
C. Both are made of cells  
D. Both are made of plant cells  

**Two parts to the question:**

1. How are they similar?  
2. What is one way they are different?

**Answer:**
1. C. Both are made of cells  
2. One is unicellular, one is multicellular.

**Guiding Question (between attempt 1 and 2 only):**  
"Think back to the fish scale and the leaf. How were they different, and how were they similar?"

**If passed (both parts correct):**  
"You just showed you understand what makes a cell important in life. You know how cells build organisms like amoebas and trees, and how they are alike and different. You’re thinking like a real scientist!"

**If failed after 2 attempts:**  
“This is a tricky one – it tests your understanding of what a cell is, how organisms are built, and how similar and different they can be. We’ll come back to it next time – you’ve already made great progress!”

**Alternate Capstone (for retry):**

**Problem**:  
Luis draws a diagram of a plant and an animal cell. What part should he include in **both** types of cells?

A. Cell Wall  
B. Chloroplast  
C. Nucleus  
D. Mitochondria  

**Answer**: C. Nucleus  
**Explanation**: The nucleus is found in all cells (plant, animal, fungal, bacterial), where it controls the activities and stores genetic information.
