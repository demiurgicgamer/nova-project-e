## Stage 1: Hook
### Story 1: The School Play Fundraiser
Emma is helping her school organize a fundraising event for a new stage. The school needs to raise $850 total. So far, they’ve collected $250 from ticket sales. Emma’s group has decided to sell special cookies to raise the rest. If they sell each cookie for $2 and their goal is to raise exactly $850, how many cookies must they sell?

**Nova (Narrator):**
"The school group is working hard to find the right number of cookies to bake. The equation they can use is: 250 + 2 × (number of cookies) = 850. This equation will help them figure out how many cookies they must sell to make their goal! How can we solve for the number of cookies?"

### Story 2: The Road Trip
Jake is going on a road trip from Toronto to Vancouver with his family. The total driving distance is 4,200 kilometers. They have already driven 1,800 km. They plan to drive at an average speed of 80 km/h. How many more hours will they have to drive to reach Vancouver? They can use an equation similar to this: 1,800 + 80 × (number of hours) = 4,200. Can you help Jake and his family find out how many more hours they need to drive?

**Nova (Narrator):**
"Jake is getting excited because he's learning how to plan his trip using math. Just like Emma, he has to solve an equation to find out how long the final stretch of the journey will take. These equations help us solve real problems in our everyday lives — like getting to your destination or planning a fundraiser."

### Objective:
To introduce the concept of linear equations in the context of real-world problems — helping the child understand that equations represent meaningful relationships in daily life.

## Stage 2: Conceptual Understanding
### Comprehension Check:

**Nova (Narrator):**
"Let's see if you get the idea. What is the main idea of a linear equation, and how can it be used to solve problems like raising funds or calculating time?"

**a)** A linear equation is used only for adding numbers  
**b)** A linear equation is a statement showing the relationship between known and unknown values  
**c)** A linear equation shows how many times something has happened  
**d)** A linear equation helps you to make a graph  

**Nova (Narrator):**
"You’ve seen real examples with cookies and road trips — linear equations give a clear way to describe problems where we know some values and need to find one unknown value."

### Interventions:
- **Level 1:** "Think of the problem step by step — what do we know, and what are we trying to find?"
- **Level 2:** "A linear equation tells us the value of something we want to know, using numbers and operations."
- **Level 3:** "What makes an equation linear? It has one variable, usually represented as 'x', and the highest power of that variable is 1."

### Objective:
To understand that linear equations represent relationships between known quantities and unknown variables, using inverse operations to solve for the variable.

## Stage 3: Guided Problem Solving
### The Cookie Fundraiser Problem (from Story 1):

**Nova (Narrator):**
"Let’s walk through how to find how many cookies they need to sell using the equation: 250 + 2 × x = 850, where x is the number of cookies. Let’s solve this step-by-step. What should we do first?"

**a)** Add 250 to both sides  
**b)** Subtract 250 from both sides  
**c)** Divide both sides by 2  
**d)** Multiply both sides by 2  

**Nova (Narrator):**
"Good job. To solve for x, we must get x by itself. So we remove the 250 that is added to it first."

### Interventions:
- **Level 1:** "What operation is being used with 250 in the equation? How can we undo that?"
- **Level 2:** "We use inverse operations to cancel. Since 250 is being added, subtracting 250 from both sides will cancel it out."
- **Level 3:** "Let’s try subtracting 250 from both sides and simplify: 250 + 2x – 250 = 850 – 250. What remains on the left side?"

## Stage 4: Practice Problems

### Problem 1: The Road Trip (from Story 2)
**Nova (Narrator):**
"Jake and his family are planning their remaining portion of the journey. Their total distance is 4,200 km, and they already drove 1,800 km. If they plan to drive at 80 km/h, how many more hours will it take them to finish the trip? The equation is: 1,800 + 80 × y = 4,200. What is the value of y?"

**a)** y = 30 hours  
**b)** y = 20 hours  
**c)** y = 15 hours  
**d)** y = 40 hours  

### Interventions:
- **Level 1:** "How do you get the variable by itself in this equation?"
- **Level 2:** "Subtract 1,800 from both sides before dividing by 80."
- **Level 3:** "Subtract 1,800 from both sides: 80y = 4,200 – 1,800. Then divide by 80 to find y."

### Problem 2: The Water Tank
**Nova (Narrator):**
"The school is building a water tank with a capacity of 600 liters. They already filled it with 200 liters. If water is being added at a rate of 50 liters per minute, how long will it take to fill the tank completely? The equation is: 200 + 50 × t = 600. What is the value of t?"

**a)** t = 5 minutes  
**b)** t = 8 minutes  
**c)** t = 10 minutes  
**d)** t = 6 minutes  

### Interventions:
- **Level 1:** "How would you start solving for t in this equation?"
- **Level 2:** "Think: What step comes after removing the 200 from the left side?"
- **Level 3:** "Subtract both sides: 50t = 600 – 200. Then divide both sides by 50."

### Problem 3: The Library Books
**Nova (Narrator):**
"The library has 700 books in total. It already has 300 books in the children's section. If they are putting 40 more books into the children's section each week, how many weeks will it take to have 700 children's books? Equation: 300 + 40 × w = 700. What is w?"

**a)** w = 10 weeks  
**b)** w = 15 weeks  
**c)** w = 12 weeks  
**d)** w = 8 weeks  

### Interventions:
- **Level 1:** "Can you identify what part of the equation needs to be removed first?"
- **Level 2:** "What is the inverse operation of adding 300?"
- **Level 3:** "Subtract 300 from both sides: 40w = 700 – 300. Then divide both sides by 40."

## Stage 5: Capstone

### Problem:
**Nova (Narrator):**
"Let’s bring it all together. Consider a problem from a real-life situation: A bike rental shop charges a flat fee of $10 and then $5 per hour. If someone rents a bike and spends exactly $35, how many hours did they use it for? Let x represent the number of hours. Which equation can we use, and what is the value of x?"

**a)** Equation: 10 + 5x = 35, x = 5 hours  
**b)** Equation: 10x + 5 = 35, x = 3 hours  
**c)** Equation: 10 + 5 + x = 35, x = 20 hours  
**d)** Equation: 10 – 5x = 35, x = 5 hours  

### Interventions:
- **Level 1:** "What are the known values and what do they represent in the equation?"
- **Level 2:** "The $10 is a fixed cost, and the $5 is multiplied by the number of hours (x). What steps do we take to solve for x?"
- **Level 3:** "Subtract 10 from both sides: 5x = 35 – 10. Then divide by 5."

### Objective:
Apply the skills learned (setting up equations and using inverse operations) to solve a full, complex real-world problem.

### Capstone Feedback:
Nova (Narrator): "Great job! You’ve learned how to solve linear equations with one variable and applied them to situations like fundraising, travel, and rentals. This knowledge can help you understand many real-world problems you’ll encounter in life."
