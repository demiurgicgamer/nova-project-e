## Stage 1 – Hook
**Story:**
Sarah and her family are shopping for back-to-school supplies. They see a great sale: "25% off all school supplies!" Sarah is curious—what does 25% mean? She looks at the price tags and sees that a pencil case originally costs $8. The sale says it's now 25% off. Sarah wants to know: How much money do they save?

Then she sees a poster for a local community event saying, "We're raising 100% of the target fund. That means we're done!" but she wonders, what exactly does it mean to raise 100% of something?

The teacher also shows a chart on the board with test scores: "40 out of 50 students passed their math test. That's 80% of the class!" 

Sarah feels her brain start to buzz with questions. What does 100% mean exactly? What about 25% or 80%? And how do these relate to the fraction she’s already been learning, like 1/4?

Let’s find out!

**Learning Goals:**
- Percent means “per hundred”
- Percent is a special type of fraction
- Use real-life examples to understand “percent”

## Stage 2 – Concept
**Story:**
Back in class, Sarah learns from her teacher that percent means "per hundred". The symbol is % and it’s the same as a fraction with a denominator of 100. So 25% is the same as 25/100.

The teacher writes:
- 25% = 25/100 = 1/4
- 100% = 100/100 = 1
- 50% = 50/100 = 1/2

She asks, “How do we turn a percent into a decimal?” Sarah thinks and says, “Well, if it’s 25% of 100, that’s 0.25.” The teacher nods and writes:
- 25% = 0.25
- 100% = 1.00
- 50% = 0.50

Sarah notices that percent is like a code:
- 70% = ?
- 15% = ?

She’s a little confused at first, but then gets a better sense of how the math works.

**Comprehension Check:**
*Question:*
What is 60% as a fraction in simplest form?

A. 3/5  
B. 6/10  
C. 60/100  
D. 30/50  

**Interventions (if needed):**

Level 1:  
“Think about what 60% means: 60 out of 100. Now, simplify!”

Level 2:  
"Try dividing both numerator and denominator by their GCD (greatest common divisor)."

Level 3:  
"What is the greatest number that can go into both 60 and 100 evenly?"

**Answer:** A. 3/5

## Stage 3 – Guided Practice

*Problem:*
A school fundraiser collects 90% of the target amount in the first week. If the goal is $5000, how much money was raised?

A. $900  
B. $4500  
C. $4000  
D. $500  

**Interventions:**

Level 1:  
"Remember that 90% = 90/100. You're looking for 90/100 of $5000."

Level 2:  
"Try solving it as a fraction: (90/100) × 5000."

Level 3:  
"Divide the total by 100 and multiply by 90."

**Answer:** B. $4500

## Stage 4 – Practice

*Problem 1:*
What is 120% as a decimal?

A. 1.2  
B. 12.0  
C. 0.12  
D. 120.0  

**Interventions:**

Level 1:  
"Remember that percent means parts per 100. So 120% = 120/100 = 1.2."

Level 2:  
"Just move the decimal point two places to the left."

Level 3:  
"Think: 120% of what number would equal 120? That gives you a clue about the decimal."

**Answer:** A. 1.2

*Problem 2:*
Convert 3/5 to a percentage.

A. 35%  
B. 60%  
C. 65%  
D. 30%  

**Interventions:**

Level 1:  
"Think: if you have 3 parts out of 5, how many parts out of 100?"

Level 2:  
"Convert 3/5 so that the denominator is 100."

Level 3:  
"Divide 3 by 5 to get a decimal, then turn that decimal into a percentage."

**Answer:** B. 60%

*Problem 3:*
If 40% of the students in a grade are boys, and there are 60 students total, how many are boys?

A. 24  
B. 40  
C. 15  
D. 30  

**Interventions:**

Level 1:  
"40% = 40/100. You want 40/100 of 60."

Level 2:  
"Think: divide 60 by 100, then multiply by 40."

Level 3:  
"40% of 60 = 40 × 60 / 100. Do that multiplication and division."

**Answer:** A. 24

## Stage 5 – Capstone

*Problem:*
A local coffee shop offers a 15% discount on all drinks. If a large coffee costs $4.00, how much will a customer pay after the discount?

A. $0.60  
B. $3.40  
C. $4.60  
D. $4.00  

**Interventions:**

Level 1:  
"First, find 15% of $4.00. Then subtract that from $4.00."

Level 2:  
"Calculate 15% × $4.00 = ? Then subtract from $4.00."

Level 3:  
"Think of 15% as a fraction or decimal, then use that to find the discount."

**Answer:** B. $3.40

**Guiding Question (between attempt 1 and attempt 2 only):**  
"What portion of the coffee’s price was taken off? How much does the customer pay for the rest?"

**If passed:**  
"Well done! You just used percentages to solve a real-life problem. You understood how percent relates to fractions and decimals, and applied that understanding to calculate a discount."

**If failed after 2 attempts:**  
"This problem had a few steps and that’s okay. You're building great math muscles. We’ll come back to it next time. You’ve done a great job today!"

**Alternate Capstone:**
A class of 30 students participated in a science fair. 25 people got a gold medal. What percentage of students won a gold medal?

A. 25%  
B. 20%  
C. 83.3%  
D. 75%  

**Guiding Question:**  
"How many out of 30 students got gold medals? Can you write that as a percentage?"
