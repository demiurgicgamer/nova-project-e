## Stage 1: Hook

### Story 1: The Mysterious Number Box

You're at the local community fair and there's a new game called The Mysterious Number Box. It works like this:
- A number is hidden inside a box.
- Someone gives you a clue that involves a mystery number - let's call it "n."
- The clue says: "I'm thinking of a number. If I double it and add 5, I get 17."

If we use the letter 'n' to stand for the unknown number, then the clue can be written as: **2n + 5 = 17**

The question is: What number is hidden in the box?

### Story 2: Sharing the Pies

At school, you have a class bake sale! You’re in charge of making sure everyone gets a fair share.

There are 12 cupcakes to be shared equally among the students. If we call the number of students in your class "s", then how many cupcakes does everyone get?

The answer is: 12 ÷ s (or 12/s)

This is a case where we have a **variable** (s) standing in for something that can change - like the number of students who show up! 

So, if 4 students show up, everyone gets 12 ÷ 4 = 3 cupcakes. But if 6 students show up, everyone gets 6 cupcakes.

**Both stories introduce the idea that letters can be used to stand for numbers that we don’t yet know — that’s what “variables” mean!**

## Stage 2: Concept

### Introduction to Variables

We're going to learn how to use letters like **n**, **s**, or **x** to stand in for mystery numbers.

These letters are called **variables** because they can **vary** — or change.

Here’s how we work with them:

- **Writing expressions with variables**: 
  - If we have 3 apples and some unknown number (let's call it x) of oranges, we write it as: 3 + x.
  
- **Evaluating expressions**:  
  If x = 5, then 3 + x = 3 + 5 = 8.

- **Substituting values**:  
  If someone says x = 8, we put 8 where we see x in the expression.

### Comprehension Check:

**What does the expression 4x + 2 mean when x = 3?**

A) 4 + 2 = 6  
B) 4 × 3 + 2 = 14  
C) 4 + 3 + 2 = 9  
D) 4 × 3 × 2 = 24  

**Interventions (only if incorrect):**

**Level 1:** Try plugging 3 in for x, then calculate step by step.
**Level 2:** Remember that multiplying comes before adding.
**Level 3:** You want to calculate 4 × 3 + 2. 4 × 3 = 12, then 12 + 2 = 14.

## Stage 3: Guided

### Problem

A teacher puts some pencils into boxes. She knows that she had 5 pencils per box and that she filled "b" boxes.

**What is an expression for the total number of pencils?**

A) 5 + b  
B) 5 - b  
C) 5 × b  
D) 5 ÷ b  

**Interventions (if answer is incorrect):**

**Level 1:** Think of how many pencils in each box and how many boxes there are.
**Level 2:** Multiplication tells us how many when you have equal groups.
**Level 3:** If there are 5 pencils in EACH box and b boxes, how many are there all together? You multiply them!

## Stage 4: Practice

### Problem 1

If y = 7, what is the value of 3y - 2?

A) 21 - 2 = 19  
B) 3 + 7 - 2 = 8  
C) 3 × 7 - 2 = 19  
D) 3 × 7 × 2 = 42  

**Interventions:**

**Level 1:** Plug in 7 for y and go step by step.
**Level 2:** Order of operations: multiply before subtracting.
**Level 3:** 3y = 3 × 7 = 21. Then 21 - 2 = 19.

---

### Problem 2

Which expression best matches: "Three times a number divided by four"?

A) 3n + 4  
B) 3n ÷ 4  
C) 3 + n ÷ 4  
D) 3n - 4  

**Interventions:**

**Level 1:** Break it down: "a number" = n, "three times" = 3n, "divided by four" = ÷ 4.
**Level 2:** "Three times" means multiplication and "divided by" means division.
**Level 3:** You wrote "three times a number" = 3n. This should be 3n ÷ 4.

---

### Problem 3

Solve the one-step equation: 4x = 20

A) x = 24  
B) x = 5  
C) x = 16  
D) x = 80  

**Interventions:**

**Level 1:** Think: what number, when multiplied by 4, gives 20?
**Level 2:** Division works to "undo" multiplication.
**Level 3:** 4x = 20 → x = 20 ÷ 4 = 5

## Stage 5: Capstone

### Problem

A recipe for cookies needs 3 cups of flour. So far, the chef has added 1 cup.

How much more flour does he need?

And what fraction of the total flour has he already added?

**First Part:**  
The amount of flour still needed = 3 - 1 = 2 cups. So, 2 cups more.

**Second Part:**  
The fraction already added = 1 ÷ 3 = 1/3

A) 1 cup more, and 1/3 of total used  
B) 2 cups more, and 1/3 of total used  
C) 1 cup more, and 1/4 of total used  
D) 2 cups more, and 2/3 of total used  

**Interventions (only between first and second attempt):**

**Level 1:** What’s the total? What’s what’s been used so far? Start there.
**Level 2:** Subtract to find how much is left. Divide to find the fraction.
**Level 3:** Total = 3 cups. Already used = 1 cup. Left = 3 - 1 = 2 cups. Fraction = 1 ÷ 3 = 1/3.
