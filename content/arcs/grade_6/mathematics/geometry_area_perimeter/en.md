## Stage 1: Hook
### Story: Fence Me In – A Canadian Garden Story

It's springtime in Maple Creek, Ontario, and Mrs. Jackson is planning to build a new fence around her triangular garden. She needs to know how much fencing material to buy. Her garden has sides measuring 10 meters, 12 meters, and 15 meters.

Meanwhile, in Vancouver, Mr. Chen is planning a new rectangular garden in his backyard. His garden is 20 meters long and 15 meters wide.

Mrs. Jackson and Mr. Chen have both done their homework, but they are getting conflicting advice from different garden suppliers. Who can help them calculate the correct amounts of fencing needed?

As we explore, you'll discover the difference between how you measure the edge and how you measure the space inside a shape.

### Story: Floor Plan Fever – A Toronto Apartment Story

Tina has just moved to Toronto and is redecorating her new apartment. She needs to know how much carpet she’ll need to cover the floor of her rectangular living room (12 feet long and 9 feet wide). Meanwhile, her kitchen is a square with sides of 6 feet.

Tina also wants to install new trim around her bedroom, which is also rectangular, 8 feet long by 5 feet wide.

She needs the space in her apartment to be both functional and beautiful – but first, she has to calculate what she needs to buy. What does she need for carpet, and what does she need for trim?

We are going to help her figure out how much area and perimeter she needs!

### Key Concept (to be introduced in Stage 2):
- **Perimeter** is the distance around the outside of a shape.
- **Area** is the space inside a shape.

## Stage 2: Concept
### Objective:
Introduce the concepts of perimeter and area for basic shapes.

### Comprehension Check:
**Question:** Is the perimeter the space inside the shape, or the distance around the outside?
A. The space inside
B. The distance around the outside
C. The volume of the shape
D. It's the same thing as area

**Correct Answer:** B

### Interventions:
**Level 1:** "If you're walking around the edge of a soccer field, what are you measuring? Inside or outside?"
**Level 2:** "Think about it this way: perimeter is like fencing a garden – you go around the outside edge. Area is like putting grass or carpet inside the garden."
**Level 3:** "Can you find a shape and tell me the distance around the edges vs. the space inside it?"

## Stage 3: Guided
### Problem:
A rectangular garden in Ottawa is 12 meters long and 8 meters wide.

**Part 1:** What is the perimeter of the garden?

A. 40 square meters  
B. 96 square meters  
C. 40 meters  
D. 96 meters  

**Answer:** C

**Interventions:**
**Level 1:** "How many sides does a rectangle have? What is the formula for perimeter?"
**Level 2:** "We know the length is 12 meters and width is 8 meters. Can you add them up and double it?"
**Level 3:** "Perimeter = 2 × (length + width) = 2 × (12 + 8). Try calculating that."

**Part 2:** What is the area of the garden?

A. 96 square meters  
B. 96 meters  
C. 40 meters  
D. 40 square meters  

**Answer:** A

**Interventions:**
**Level 1:** "What is the area formula for rectangles?"
**Level 2:** "Area = length × width. What do you get when you multiply 12 × 8?"
**Level 3:** "So 12 meters × 8 meters = 96 square meters."

### Problem 2:  
A triangle-shaped park in Montreal has a base of 12 meters and a height of 8 meters. What is the area?

A. 96 square meters  
B. 48 square meters  
C. 96 meters  
D. 20 square meters  

**Answer:** B

**Interventions:**
**Level 1:** "What is the area formula for a triangle?"
**Level 2:** "Try ½ × base × height = ½ × 12 × 8."
**Level 3:** "So ½ × 12 × 8 = 48 square meters."

### Problem 3:  
A composite shape made of a rectangle (10 cm long, 6 cm wide) and a semicircle (radius 3 cm), attached to one of the shorter sides of the rectangle. What is the approximate area of the composite shape? (Use π ≈ 3.14)

A. 94.4 square cm  
B. 188.4 square cm  
C. 60 square cm  
D. 34.4 square cm  

**Answer:** A

**Interventions:**
**Level 1:** "What are the shapes in the composite? How do you find the area of each?"
**Level 2:** "RectArea = 10 × 6 = 60 square cm. What is the area of the semicircle?"
**Level 3:** "Area of semicircle = ½ × π × r² = ½ × 3.14 × 3². Add it to rectangle area."

## Stage 5: Capstone
### Problem:
A Canadian landscape architect is planning a new playground in a park shaped like a composite figure. It includes a rectangle and two semicircles on opposite sides.

- Rectangle dimensions: 20 meters long, 10 meters wide  
- Semicircles: Radius = 5 meters each  

**Part 1:** What is the total area of the playground (use π ≈ 3.14)?

A. 200 square meters  
B. 257 square meters  
C. 357 square meters  
D. 57 square meters  

**Answer:** C

**Interventions:**
**Level 1:** "What two shapes are in this figure? How do you find the area of each?"
**Level 2:** "Rectangle area = 20 × 10 = 200. Two semicircles form one full circle. Circle = π × r² = 3.14 × 25. What’s the total now?"
**Level 3:** "Total area = rectangle + 1 circle = 200 + (3.14 × 25)."

**Part 2:** What is the approximate perimeter of the playground?

A. 50 meters  
B. 70 meters  
C. 90 meters  
D. 100 meters  

**Answer:** B

**Interventions:**
**Level 1:** "What parts of the shape’s edge make up the perimeter?"
**Level 2:** "The two longer sides of the rectangle, plus the outer arcs of the semicircles. What do the semicircles contribute?"
**Level 3:** "Two semicircles = one full circle. Circumference = 2πr = 2 × 3.14 × 5 = ? Add to rectangle sides."
