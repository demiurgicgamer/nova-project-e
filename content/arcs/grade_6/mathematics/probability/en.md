## Stage 1: Hook
**Objective:** Introduce children to the idea of probability in natural, relatable contexts. 

### Hook Story – "Lucky Day at the Fair":
At [a Canadian fair in Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_Fair), you're spinning a wheel to win a prize. The wheel has 4 sections:

- Blue (1/4 of the wheel)
- Red (1/4 of the wheel)
- Green (1/4 of the wheel)
- Yellow (1/4 of the wheel)

You spin the wheel and it lands on **Red**. You think, “I’m really lucky!” But then, a friend says, “No, I spun it too and it landed on Red.” You both wonder – is it really that lucky, or does it happen often?

*Why this story works:*
- The scenario is familiar from Canada, especially during summer festivals.
- It introduces the idea of events with different probabilities.
- Encourages conversation about chance.

### Hook Story – "The Weather Forecast":
In [Vancouver, British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver), a weather app predicts there is a 2/3 chance of rain tomorrow. A little girl sees this and says, “It’s going to rain a lot!” Her grandmother, used to weather reports, says, “No, it’s not that likely. It just means it’s more likely to rain than not.” 

*Why this story works:*
- The setting is relevant to kids in Canada’s west coast.
- It helps bridge formal probability with everyday language.
- Introduces both the language of probability and how it is interpreted.

### Discussion Prompts:
1. Why might some events be more or less likely than others?
2. If something can happen 1 out of 4 times, what fraction of the time might it happen?
3. Can you think of a time you've felt lucky?

## Stage 2: Concept
**Objective:** Introduce basic probability as a fraction between 0 and 1, with terminology like certain, impossible, likely, unlikely.

### Concept: Probability as a Fraction
- Probability can be shown as a **fraction from 0 to 1**.
    - **0** = impossible (Event will never happen)
    - **1** = certain (Event will always happen)
    - **0.5** or **1/2** = equally likely (Event has equal chance of happening or not happening)
- **Likely** events have a high probability (close to 1).
- **Unlikely** events have a low probability (close to 0).

### Concept Checkpoint
**Problem: You pick a card from a deck of 52 cards (which has 4 aces). What fraction of the cards are aces? What does this tell you about how likely you are to pick an ace?**

#### Choices:
A. 1/4, Likely  
B. 1/52, Unlikely  
C. 4/52 = 1/13, Unlikely  
D. 4/52 = 1/13, Likely  

#### Correct Answer:
C. 4/52 = 1/13, Unlikely

#### Intervention Hints:
**Level 1:** "How many aces are in a deck of cards? What's the total number of cards?"

**Level 2:** "Think about this: if 4 out of 52 cards are aces, that’s a very small number compared to the rest. Is that likely or unlikely?"

**Level 3:** "Probability = (Number of favorable outcomes) / (Total number of outcomes). How many aces? How many cards in total?"

## Stage 3: Guided
**Objective:** Build confidence in identifying probabilities and matching real-world outcomes.

### Guided Problem: The Coin Toss Game
You have a coin. When you flip it:
- It lands on **Heads** or **Tails**
- Each outcome is **equally likely**
- The probability of either is **1/2**

Now, imagine you’re playing a game where you get a point if the coin lands on **Heads**.

**Problem: What is the probability of getting a point in one flip?**

#### Choices:
A. 0  
B. 1/2  
C. 1  
D. 2/1  

#### Correct Answer:
B. 1/2

#### Intervention Hints:
**Level 1:** "How many sides does a coin have?" 

**Level 2:** "We flip the coin once – what are the possible outcomes?"

**Level 3:** "Probability = favorable outcomes/total outcomes. One way to get a point, two total outcomes. What fraction is that?"

## Stage 4: Practice
**Objective:** Solidify understanding through guided practice with increasingly challenging scenarios.

### Practice Problem 1: Rolling a Die
You roll a six-sided die.
The die shows numbers 1 to 6, each with the same chance.
What is the probability of rolling a 3?

#### Choices:
A. 1/6  
B. 1/3  
C. 1/2  
D. 1  

#### Correct Answer:
A. 1/6

#### Intervention Hints:
**Level 1:** "How many faces does a die have? What number are you looking for?"

**Level 2:** "If each side has an equal chance, how much chance is there of landing on one specific side?"

**Level 3:** "Think about all outcomes – how many sides are there? How many sides show a 3?"

---

### Practice Problem 2: Drawing from a Bag
There are 10 balls in a bag:
- 3 are red
- 4 are blue
- 3 are green

If you pick a ball at random, what is the probability of picking a blue ball?

#### Choices:
A. 3/10  
B. 4/10 = 2/5  
C. 1/10  
D. 1/4  

#### Correct Answer:
B. 4/10 = 2/5

#### Intervention Hints:
**Level 1:** "How many total balls are in the bag? How many are blue?"

**Level 2:** "Is the chance of picking a blue ball higher than a red ball?"

**Level 3:** "Probability = number of blue balls / total balls. What fraction is that?"

---

### Practice Problem 3: The Spinner
You spin this spinner that’s divided into 8 equal sections:
- 2 sections are Blue
- 3 sections are Red
- 2 sections are Green
- 1 section is Yellow

What is the probability of landing on Red?

#### Choices:
A. 3/8  
B. 2/8 = 1/4  
C. 1/8  
D. 3/16  

#### Correct Answer:
A. 3/8

#### Intervention Hints:
**Level 1:** "How many sections are in the spinner? How many are red?"

**Level 2:** "It’s a fair spinner – each section has an equal chance. How much chance is there for landing on red?"

**Level 3:** "Probability = red sections / total sections. What’s the simplest way to express that fraction?"

## Stage 5: Capstone
**Objective:** Apply understanding to a multi-step real-world problem involving theoretical probability.

### Capstone Problem: The School Raffle
For the school fair, you're helping organize a raffle. There are 50 tickets in the raffle.
- 10 of the 50 tickets are winning tickets.
- All tickets are equally likely to win.

**Question 1:** What is the probability that if you buy one ticket, you win a prize?

#### Choices:
A. 1/5  
B. 10/50 = 1/5  
C. 40/50 = 4/5  
D. 50/10 = 5  

#### Correct Answer:
B. 10/50 = 1/5

#### Question 2: If 30 tickets are sold in the first round and 25 of them are winners in that round, what is the experimental probability of drawing a winner from that round?

#### Choices:
A. 25/30 = 5/6  
B. 30/25 = 6/5  
C. 5/30 = 1/6  
D. 25/50 = 1/2  

#### Correct Answer:
A. 25/30 = 5/6

#### Intervention Hints:
**Q1:**
- **Level 1:** "How many total tickets are there? How many are winning tickets?"
- **Level 2:** "If you have 10 winning tickets out of 50, that’s a fraction – what is it?"
- **Level 3:** "Probability = winning tickets / total tickets. What's the fraction and is it simplified?"

**Q2:**
- **Level 1:** "What number of tickets were sold? How many were winners in that round?"
- **Level 2:** "If 25 out of 30 are winners, what's the fraction?"
- **Level 3:** "Experimental probability = number of successes / number of trials. How would that be applied here?"

## Summary:
This probability arc introduces foundational ideas of probability as a **fraction**, connects them to **real-life situations**, and builds up to applying concepts in **multi-step tasks**. Each phase reinforces learning through story, discussion, guided questions, and practical application.

--- 

**Note:** Students are expected to understand **probability as a fraction between 0 and 1**, **the interpretation of those fractions** (certain, likely, unlikely, impossible), and **the difference between theoretical and experimental probability**. The arc includes language and examples from real Canadian settings.
